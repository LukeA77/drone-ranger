# Limitations

*Measured limits are filled from `results/`; every limit here is also reported in the brief.*

## The main gap: no end-to-end test with known object distances

No single test runs detect, track and range end to end against known object distances. Tracking is scored on VisDrone (boxes, no distances), ranging on Mid-Air (distances, no objects; exact poses) and real footage on AU-AIR (neither true distances nor a well-transferred detector). The box-to-ground simulation and the pose-noise sweep narrow the gap but are still separate pieces. The most valuable next step is one setup where every object's distance is known: a drone flown past a car carrying a GPS logger, or a simulator with vehicles and people (for example CARLA), scored through the whole pipeline.

## Distance estimation (ranging)

### Measured on the Mid-Air test flights (synthetic, run once)

- **Flat ground fails in practice.** With the true height above the ground plane (an unrealistic best case), the median distance error is {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_flat_median|.1%}} on the {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_share|.0%}} of samples it shares with terrain from motion; with a take-off altimeter (realistic) it is {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.flat_alt_median|.0%}} over its own valid estimates, which are only {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.flat_alt_valid|.0%}} of samples. On one test flight the altimeter method had no valid estimate at all: the drone flew below its take-off height (D-607).
- **Slopes (hypothesis H4).** Flat-ground error grows from {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.flat_err_slope_0_5|.1%}} on slopes under 5° to {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.flat_err_slope_45_90|.1%}} on slopes over 45°.
- **Near the horizon (H2).** Below 2° of depression the error distribution becomes strongly non-linear: the Monte Carlo prediction is {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.mc_over_fo_below_2deg|.1f}} times the first-order one, so first-order error bars are not trustworthy there.
- **Terrain from motion still has a heavy tail, and does not win everywhere.** On the {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_share|.0%}} of samples it shares with the flat plane its median error is {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_terrain_median|.1%}}, but one estimate in ten is more than {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_terrain_p90|.0%}} off. Over all of its own valid estimates ({{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.terrain_valid|.0%}} of land samples, including harder ones the flat plane cannot range at all) the figures are higher: median {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.terrain_median|.1%}}, one in ten more than {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.terrain_p90|.0%}} off. On the samples it shares with the flat-plane baseline it has the lower median on {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_flights_better|d}} of {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_n_flights|d}} flights; on flight {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_worst_flight|d}} it is worse ({{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_worst_flight_terrain|.1%}} against {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_worst_flight_flat|.1%}}). All intervals rest on {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.n_test_flights|d}} test flights.
- **Terrain from motion needs accurate frame-to-frame poses.** Its headline errors use exact poses. Per-frame position noise of 10 cm raises the median to {{RESULT:headline.json#robustness.pos_jitter_0p1m_terrain|.0%}}, worse than flat ground with the true height, and 0.5° of attitude noise to {{RESULT:headline.json#robustness.att_jitter_0p5deg_terrain|.0%}} (development flights, D-621). Lens distortion, rolling shutter and focal-length error are not modelled.
- **Ranging a box, not a ground point, reads cars short.** The bottom-centre of a car's box is its near edge: {{RESULT:headline.json#robustness.car_contact_median_abs|.1%}} short at the median in simulation, {{RESULT:headline.json#robustness.car_end_to_end_median|.1%}} end to end with exact poses (D-622). No accuracy on real footage has been measured.
- **Error bars fall short.** After calibration on separate flights, nominal 68 %, 90 % and 95 % intervals contain the truth {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.cov_cal_68|.0%}}, {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.cov_cal_90|.0%}} and {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.cov_cal_95|.0%}} of the time: better than without calibration, but still short (H3).
- **The reliability flag is moderate.** Area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.flag_auroc|.2f}} on test flights ({{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.flag_auroc_dev|.2f}} in development); precision {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.flag_precision|.2f}}, recall {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.flag_recall|.2f}}.

### Mid-Air scope

- Synthetic imagery from one climate, with no people or vehicles: it validates geometry, not detection.
- Intervals resample whole flights, and there are {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.n_test_flights|d}} test flights, so intervals are wide.

## Real footage (AU-AIR)

- **No ground truth.** AU-AIR records no target positions or speeds, so its results are repeatability and plausibility, never accuracy.
- **The camera model is inferred.** Focal length {{RESULT:headline.json#auair.focal_px|.0f}} pixels (95 % interval {{RESULT:headline.json#auair.focal_ci.0|.0f}} to {{RESULT:headline.json#auair.focal_ci.1|.0f}}), principal point assumed at the image centre, lens distortion not fitted (the central part of the image is used where it matters), camera tilt not recorded and estimated from motion (it agreed with independent checks where they were possible, D-514; available on {{RESULT:headline.json#auair.tilt_frame_share|.0%}} of frames, because it needs the drone to move).
- **The altimeter drifts** by metres within a flight; the offset was measured only on two calibration flights (weakly on one).
- **Shallow views.** Held-out repeatability is {{RESULT:headline.json#auair.heldout_rel_dep_20_40|.1%}} of range at 20-40° below the horizon but {{RESULT:headline.json#auair.heldout_rel_dep_0_10|.1%}} below 10°.
- **Implausible speeds.** {{RESULT:headline.json#auair.vehicle_share_above_70|.0%}} of vehicle tracks read above 70 km/h at a junction where that is implausible; likely causes (shallow views, identity switches) are not tested.
- **Detector transfer.** The VisDrone-trained detector scores poorly on AU-AIR at the protocol threshold (vehicle AP50 {{RESULT:headline.json#auair.heldout_vehicle_ap50|.2f}}, people {{RESULT:headline.json#auair.heldout_person_ap50|.2f}} on held-out flights); part of this is AU-AIR's looser annotation boxes (D-512).
- **Data:** one drone model, one site (a junction in Aarhus), heights of {{RESULT:headline.json#auair.alt_min_m|.0f}} to {{RESULT:headline.json#auair.alt_max_m|.0f}} m, short ranges. AU-AIR's annotations are not consistent from frame to frame (D-516).

## Tracking (VisDrone)

- **Small objects.** People 16 to 32 pixels tall are found {{RESULT:headline.json#visdrone_test.person_recall_16_32|.0%}} of the time, against {{RESULT:headline.json#visdrone_test.person_recall_128|.0%}} above 128 pixels.
- **The tuned tracker trades recall for fewer false tracks**: it misses more people in exchange for far fewer identity switches (D-321).
- No appearance re-identification, by design; identity switches under occlusion remain the main tracking error (failure gallery, D-321).
- Comparisons with MOT17 numbers show a domain gap and are not like-for-like.
- VisDrone states no licence; it is used for research evaluation only and not redistributed.

## Speed (edge budget)

- **One laptop CPU** ({{RESULT:headline.json#edge.cpu|s}}); no claims about other hardware.
- **30 frames per second is reached in throughput, not in delay.** The fast setting sustains {{RESULT:headline.json#edge.fast_fps|.1f}} frames per second, but each frame's result arrives {{RESULT:headline.json#edge.fast_latency_median_ms|.0f}} ms after it is read (99th percentile {{RESULT:headline.json#edge.fast_latency_p99_ms|.0f}} ms), and its tracking accuracy is lower (validation IDF1 {{RESULT:headline.json#edge.fast_idf1|.3f}} against {{RESULT:headline.json#edge.accurate_idf1|.3f}}).
- **8-bit integer (INT8) quantisation was slower** on this CPU ({{RESULT:headline.json#edge.int8dyn_detect_ms|.0f}} ms per detection with dynamic INT8 against {{RESULT:headline.json#edge.fp32_detect_ms|.0f}} ms in 32-bit floating point), so it is not used.
- Benchmarks ran with the editor open (measured background load is recorded in each benchmark file).

## Questions and answers

Short answers to the questions an engineer is likely to ask, with the evidence for each.

**How accurate is the range, and at what distances?** On the Mid-Air test flights (synthetic, so the truth is exact), terrain from motion is compared with flat ground given the true height on the same samples: on the {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_share|.0%}} of samples where both give an answer, terrain from motion has a median distance error of {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_terrain_median|.1%}} ({{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_terrain_median_ci.0|.1%}} to {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_terrain_median_ci.1|.1%}}) against {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_flat_median|.1%}} ({{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_flat_median_ci.0|.1%}} to {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_flat_median_ci.1|.1%}}) for flat ground given the true height, and a 90th percentile of {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_terrain_p90|.0%}} against {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_flat_p90|.0%}}; it is closer on {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_terrain_closer|.0%}} of samples and has the lower median on {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_flights_better|d}} of {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_n_flights|d}} flights (not on flight {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_worst_flight|d}}: {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_worst_flight_terrain|.1%}} against {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.paired_worst_flight_flat|.1%}}). The intervals come from only {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.n_test_flights|d}} flights. Over all of its own valid estimates ({{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.terrain_valid|.0%}} of land samples, including harder ones the flat plane cannot range) the figures are higher: median {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.terrain_median|.1%}} ({{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.terrain_median_ci.0|.1%}} to {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.terrain_median_ci.1|.1%}}), one in ten more than {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.terrain_p90|.0%}} off. A take-off altimeter is not a fair baseline here: the drone often flies below its take-off point, so most of its estimates are invalid or badly wrong. On real footage (AU-AIR) there is no ground truth, so only repeatability is measured: stationary vehicles' positions repeat to {{RESULT:headline.json#auair.heldout_rel_all|.1%}} of their distance. Ranges tested are short (low-altitude flight); no long-range claim is made. Evidence: `results/midair/test/terrain/`, `results/auair/repeatability_view_held_out_B2_yolo26n_960.json`.

**Those figures use perfect poses. What happens with real navigation errors?** The Mid-Air evaluation gives terrain from motion the simulator's exact poses and an ideal camera. A sweep on the {{RESULT:headline.json#robustness.n_dev_flights|d}} development flights perturbs every pose the method uses (`scripts/terrain_pose_sweep.py`). With exact poses these flights give {{RESULT:headline.json#robustness.exact_terrain|.1%}} (flat ground: {{RESULT:headline.json#robustness.exact_flat|.1%}}). Errors shared by all frames barely matter: a 5 m GPS offset leaves the median at {{RESULT:headline.json#robustness.pos_bias_5m_terrain|.1%}} (flat ground with the true height: {{RESULT:headline.json#robustness.pos_bias_5m_flat|.0%}}) and a 2° tilt error gives {{RESULT:headline.json#robustness.att_bias_2deg_terrain|.1%}}. Errors that change between frames matter a lot, because triangulation uses frames 0.04 to 0.4 s apart: 2 cm of per-frame position noise gives {{RESULT:headline.json#robustness.pos_jitter_0p02m_terrain|.1%}}, 10 cm gives {{RESULT:headline.json#robustness.pos_jitter_0p1m_terrain|.1%}} (worse than flat ground's {{RESULT:headline.json#robustness.pos_jitter_0p1m_flat|.0%}}), and 0.1° of attitude noise gives {{RESULT:headline.json#robustness.att_jitter_0p1deg_terrain|.1%}}. Three assumed navigation grades give {{RESULT:headline.json#robustness.nav_good_terrain|.1%}}, {{RESULT:headline.json#robustness.nav_typical_terrain|.1%}} and {{RESULT:headline.json#robustness.nav_poor_terrain|.0%}}. So the method needs the short-term accuracy of an inertial or visual-inertial system, not raw GNSS. Lens distortion, rolling shutter and focal-length error are not modelled. Evidence: `results/midair/development/terrain/pose_sweep.json`, D-621.

**Does ranging a detected box add error?** Yes. The Mid-Air figures range ground pixels; the pipeline ranges a box's bottom-centre, which for a vehicle seen at an angle is its near edge. Simulated cars and people on flat ground, in AU-AIR-like views with the detector's measured box noise, put cars {{RESULT:headline.json#robustness.car_contact_median_abs|.1%}} short at the median (people {{RESULT:headline.json#robustness.person_contact_median_abs|.1%}}). Combined with the ranging error, a car is {{RESULT:headline.json#robustness.car_end_to_end_median|.1%}} off at the median and more than {{RESULT:headline.json#robustness.car_end_to_end_p90|.0%}} off one time in ten, with exact poses. The car bias is systematic, so it could be corrected. Evidence: `results/ranging/contact_point_budget.json`, D-622.

**What happens near the horizon?** Errors grow quickly as the line of sight flattens. Below 2° of depression, error propagation becomes non-linear (the Monte Carlo prediction is {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.mc_over_fo_below_2deg|.1f}} times the first-order one), and on AU-AIR the repeatability worsens from {{RESULT:headline.json#auair.heldout_rel_dep_20_40|.1%}} of range at 20-40° to {{RESULT:headline.json#auair.heldout_rel_dep_0_10|.1%}} below 10°. Rays at or above the horizon are reported as invalid, never ranged. Evidence: `results/midair/test/propagation.json`, D-517.

**Does it work on slopes?** A flat plane does not: its error grows from {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.flat_err_slope_0_5|.1%}} on slopes under 5° to {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.flat_err_slope_45_90|.1%}} above 45°. Terrain from motion builds a local ground surface from tracked image features and the drone's known motion. Its error stays between {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.terrain_err_slope_first|.1%}} and {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.terrain_err_slope_30_45|.1%}} on slopes up to 45°, and rises to {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.terrain_err_slope_last|.1%}} on slopes over 45° (still well below the flat plane there). Evidence: `results/midair/test/slope.json`, `results/midair/test/terrain/binned.json`.

**Is the uncertainty honest, and how do you know?** Not fully. The error bars were checked against the truth on flights not used to fit them: after calibration (split conformal prediction, fitted on separate calibration flights), nominal 68 %, 90 % and 95 % intervals contain the truth {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.cov_cal_68|.0%}}, {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.cov_cal_90|.0%}} and {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.cov_cal_95|.0%}} of the time, against {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.cov_raw_90|.0%}} for 90 % before calibration. So they are close but still a little over-confident on the test flights. Evidence: `results/midair/test/terrain/calibration.json`, D-403.

**What does the reliability flag catch and miss?** It is a small logistic model on interpretable features (local ground roughness relative to range is the strongest). On test flights its area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC) is {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.flag_auroc|.2f}}: it flags {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.flag_recall|.0%}} of the estimates that are more than 10 % wrong, and {{RESULT:headline.json#midair_test.flag_precision|.0%}} of what it flags is wrong by that much. The large errors it does not flag are the rest, so it is a useful warning, not a guarantee. Evidence: `results/midair/test/terrain/flag.json`, D-404.

**What assumptions does the camera model make on real footage?** AU-AIR publishes no camera calibration. The focal length ({{RESULT:headline.json#auair.focal_px|.0f}} pixels) was measured from the drone's GPS motion against image motion on calibration flights and checked against car sizes; the principal point is assumed at the image centre; lens distortion is handled by using the central image only where it matters; the camera tilt is not recorded, so it is estimated from image motion (and checked against the visible horizon); the altimeter's zero drifts and was corrected where it could be measured. All of this is in `docs/auair_camera_model.md`.

**What latency, on what hardware, at what percentile?** On a laptop {{RESULT:headline.json#edge.cpu|s}} CPU, the most accurate setting runs at {{RESULT:headline.json#edge.accurate_fps|.1f}} frames per second ({{RESULT:headline.json#edge.accurate_median_ms|.0f}} ms per frame, median). The fast setting sustains {{RESULT:headline.json#edge.fast_fps|.1f}} frames per second by running detection on every second frame and overlapping detection with tracking; each frame's result then arrives {{RESULT:headline.json#edge.fast_latency_median_ms|.0f}} ms after it is read (median; 99th percentile {{RESULT:headline.json#edge.fast_latency_p99_ms|.0f}} ms). Evidence: `results/edge/pareto.json`, `results/edge/figures/pareto.png`.

**How was test data kept separate from tuning?** Every dataset has a split decided before results were seen (`results/splits.json`, D-506). Test splits ran once with `--final` after the settings were frozen; `scripts/freeze.py` recorded the hash of every setting file and the test queues stopped if any changed. Code fixes made during a test run (a crash on an empty result) changed no setting and are recorded (D-607). Evidence: `results/frozen/`, `docs/decisions.md`.

**Why not use a laser rangefinder or radar, and how would this combine with one?** This project asks how far a passive camera alone can go, and how honestly it can report its own uncertainty. An active sensor measures range far better; the natural combination is to use the camera for detection, tracking and bearing, and the rangefinder for range, with this pipeline's per-object uncertainty used to weight the two in the same Kalman filter, or to take over when the active sensor drops out.

**What would you do differently with real sensor telemetry?** Record the camera tilt and lens calibration (they had to be inferred here), use a properly referenced altitude (the altimeter drifted by metres within a flight), time-stamp camera and flight data together, and use a terrain model where one exists. Each would remove one of the largest error sources measured here.

**What is the weakest part of this work?** No single test runs detect, track and range end to end against known object distances. Tracking is scored on VisDrone (boxes, no distances), ranging on Mid-Air (distances, no objects; exact poses) and real footage on AU-AIR (neither true distances nor a well-transferred detector). The box-to-ground simulation and the pose-noise sweep narrow the gap but are still separate pieces. The most valuable next step is one setup where every object's distance is known: a drone flown past a car carrying a GPS logger, or a simulator with vehicles and people (for example CARLA), scored through the whole pipeline. Beyond that: on real footage there is no ground truth for range or speed, so the real-footage results are repeatability and plausibility only, and {{RESULT:headline.json#auair.vehicle_share_above_70|.0%}} of vehicle speeds are implausibly high. On the tracking side, small people are rarely tracked ({{RESULT:headline.json#visdrone_test.person_recall_16_32|.0%}} at 16 to 32 pixels). And 30 frames per second is reached only in throughput, at a cost in accuracy, not in per-frame delay.
