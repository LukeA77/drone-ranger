# Multi-drone search simulation (Phases 14 and 16)

Several drones search an area for one missing person. Each keeps its own map of where the person may be, flies where that map says a look is worth most, and shares what it has looked at with the others over an unreliable radio. The question (hypothesis H12): does sharing observations find the person sooner and cover less ground twice than drones searching on their own, and does it still help when radio packets are lost?

The camera in the simulation is not assumed. Its chance of seeing a person, and how often it raises a false alarm, are measured from the real pipeline's output on SynDrone (Phase 10), at the same heights, camera angles and speed. Design and every choice: D-752 in [decisions.md](decisions.md).

Phase 14 counted a find at the first detection and treated false alarms as free. [Phase 16](#phase-16-confirmed-finds-sweep-first-then-look-again-compact-sharing), below, counts a find only once the team has confirmed a place for the crew, models false alarms that come back, and adds a planner and sharing built for that; its recommendation replaces Phase 14's.

## The world

- **Where the person is likely to be.** Lost hikers in temperate mountains: a quarter are found within 1.1 km of where they were last seen, half within 3.1 km, three quarters within 5.8 km and 95 % within 18.3 km (Koester, 2010; 568 cases). Each ring's share is spread evenly over it, as in the ring model studied by Sava et al. (2016). The search area is the square around the last-seen point reaching the first ring (4.8 km2). A flat prior is also run, to check that results do not depend on this choice.
- **Ground.** Flat and open. Forest, which hides people from a colour camera, is not modelled.
- **One person, standing still.**

## The drones and their camera

- **Flight.** SynDrone's three camera set-ups (20 m looking 30 degrees down, 50 m at 60 degrees, 80 m straight down) at 21.9 m/s, the speed of the SynDrone flights. Only ground at least 10 degrees below the horizon counts as searched.
- **Seeing the person.** Measured per pass (a stretch of time a person stays in view): the share of passes in which the tracker matched them. It rises steeply with how long they stay in view.
- **False alarms.** Measured per km2 of ground searched.
- **Two measured cameras.** The Phase 10 test towns (mostly streets) and Town07, the most rural SynDrone town.
- **Output.** Detections leave the camera model as the ROS 2 node's `TrackedObjectArray` messages and are read back by the same code the map uses.

## The search map and sharing

Each drone's map holds, for every 10 m cell, the probability that the person is there. A pass that sees nothing lowers a cell by the chance it would have seen someone; a detection raises its cell, by less where false alarms are common (Bayesian search, Koopman 1946; Stone 1975). Drones share their observations (which cells they passed, how well, and what they detected), not their maps. Each observation has a unique number, so a drone that receives one twice, or receives them in a different order, ends with the same map. Observations are passed on from drone to drone, so they reach drones out of each other's radio range.

The radio reaches drones within a set range, loses each packet with a set probability, and allows a set number of bytes per second. All three are swept rather than fixed at one guessed value.

## Planners

- **Lawnmower.** Each drone sweeps its own strip in parallel lanes, again and again. Needs no radio.
- **Greedy.** Each drone flies to the block of ground where it expects to find the person fastest, by its own map.
- **Auction.** As greedy, but each drone announces its choice; a drone that hears a better claim for its block chooses again.

The planner and its block size are chosen on development runs (seeds 0 to 199) by a rule fixed beforehand, then frozen; the comparison is run once on separate test seeds (10000 to 10299).

## What this means

**H12 (sharing observations reduces time to find and repeated coverage compared with independent search, and degrades gracefully with packet loss): partly supported.** Against the same planner without sharing, all three parts hold: sharing finds the person sooner at every team size and with both cameras, repeats less ground (slightly), and falls back no further than not sharing as packets are lost. It does not degrade gracefully when bandwidth is short: below the bytes it produces, its advantage soon disappears (development seeds, above). Against the other baseline, independent lawnmower strips, it does not: the lawnmower is faster at every team size, because the present detector's false alarms pull map-guided drones away from new ground. Map-guided search pays off only once false alarms are rare, or checked some other way before the map takes them in (development check: {{RESULT:multi_drone/design_check_p10_v2_cal.json#auction_b4_faoff_pdbylen.rmt|,.0f}} s against {{RESULT:multi_drone/design_check_p10_v2_cal.json#lawnmower_faoff_pdbylen.rmt|,.0f}} s without them). Until then, the recommended plan for a team is the lawnmower, one strip per drone.

## Limits

- One standing person, flat open ground, instantaneous turns, no wind, no battery limit.
- A crew checking detections, and the time that costs, is not modelled; false alarms are counted instead.
- Passes over the same person are treated as independent. Real repeat looks at the same spot are probably correlated, which flatters every planner that looks again.
- The camera was measured in rendered towns, mostly urban.
- The radio is a simple disc with independent losses.

## Phase 16: confirmed finds, sweep first then look again, compact sharing

Phase 14 counted a find at the first detection of the person and treated false alarms as free. A real crew cannot: every place the drones report has to be checked. Phase 16 changes the measure and the world to match, and adds a planner and a way of sharing built for them (D-764, D-765; plan in build2 Section 12.3).

```bash
python scripts/multidrone_clutter_fit.py --name p10_v2_cal --split test --allow-test   # how false alarms recur
python scripts/multidrone16_run.py design1 --workers 5    # then design2, design3 (development seeds)
python scripts/freeze.py --phase phase16
python scripts/multidrone16_run.py test --workers 5       # once
python scripts/multidrone16_paired.py test                 # paired comparisons for H13 to H16
python scripts/multidrone16_sensitivity.py --workers 5     # how much the recurrence matters (development seeds)
```

### What changed

- **The measure: time to a confirmed find.** A place becomes an alarm to the crew when the team has detected something there at least {{RESULT:multi_drone/phase16_test.json#settings.sweep_n4.k|d}} times, within {{RESULT:multi_drone/clutter_p10_v2_cal.json#radius_m|.2f}} m (five times the map's position error). The search ends when an alarm is the person. Places that become alarms but are not the person are **crew alarms**, counted up to the find. The first detection of the person is reported too.
- **False alarms that come back.** The frozen Phase 10 runs show that a false alarm is often something that looks like a person: on a later pass over a false-alarm site, another false alarm appears within 1.86 m on {{RESULT:multi_drone/clutter_p10_v2_cal.json#by_min_track_frames.1.recurrence.1.85814.q|.1%}} of passes ({{RESULT:multi_drone/clutter_p10_v2_cal.json#by_min_track_frames.1.recurrence.1.85814.later_passes|,d}} later passes). A two-kind fit says {{RESULT:multi_drone/clutter_p10_v2_cal.json#by_min_track_frames.1.recurrence.1.85814.persistent.share|.0%}} of false alarms come from fixed look-alikes that fire on {{RESULT:multi_drone/clutter_p10_v2_cal.json#by_min_track_frames.1.recurrence.1.85814.persistent.fire_per_pass|.0%}} of passes over them (`results/multi_drone/clutter_p10_v2_cal.json`). The world places those look-alikes once per search; the rate on a first pass stays Phase 14's measured rate.
- **Sweep first, then look again.** Each drone takes a strip and flies it in lanes; a drone that finishes takes the far end of the strip furthest behind, so no lane is flown twice by accident and a lost drone's lanes are taken over once it has been silent for {{RESULT:multi_drone/phase16_test.json#settings.sweep_n4.pc.lost_after_s|.0f}} s. After the sweep, the drones fly 4-lane blocks chosen by a map of where nothing has been seen, with detections left out, so look-alikes cannot pull them back (chosen on development seeds, below). Detections still feed the alarms.
- **Compact sharing.** Instead of cell lists, each drone sends its pose each second (8 bytes) and its detections (4 bytes each); every drone replays every log through the same camera model, so all maps agree. A drone does not resend what it has just heard others send.
- **A coordinator drone (option).** Drone 0 also searches, and each second broadcasts the best next lane for every drone (an optimal assignment). Drones follow it while it is fresh; if it goes quiet, they fall back to the leaderless rules.

### What Phase 16 means

With confirmed finds and recurring look-alikes, map-guided search in the Phase 14 style breaks down: the auction keeps returning to places that look like a person. The new sweep fixes that. It is as fast as the lawnmower when every drone keeps flying, and loses about half as much time as the lawnmower when one drone stops (a difference of {{RESULT:multi_drone/phase16_paired_test.json#comparisons.H15_lose_cost_sweep_minus_lawnmower.time.mean|+,.0f}} ({{RESULT:multi_drone/phase16_paired_test.json#comparisons.H15_lose_cost_sweep_minus_lawnmower.time.ci95.0|+,.0f}} to {{RESULT:multi_drone/phase16_paired_test.json#comparisons.H15_lose_cost_sweep_minus_lawnmower.time.ci95.1|+,.0f}}) s). It needs only {{RESULT:multi_drone/phase16_test.json#results.sweep_n4.bytes_per_drone_s_mean|,.1f}} bytes per drone per second, so it fits every radio budget Phase 14 tested. **The recommended plan for a team is now the sweep with compact sharing.** It costs nothing in speed, and the other drones take over a lost drone's lanes, which the lawnmower cannot do. The lawnmower remains as good when drones cannot talk to each other. The coordinator drone is optional: it did no harm, and losing it costs no more than losing any other drone, but it helped in only one of four comparisons.

The confirm rule does not solve false alarms for the crew. At four drones, the sweep raises {{RESULT:multi_drone/phase16_test.json#results.sweep_n4.crew_alarms_before_find_mean|,.0f}} crew alarms before the find, and most come from recurring look-alikes (above). A crew could not check that many. A detector with fewer false alarms, or a second look by eye before a place reaches the crew, is still needed. Phase 13's thermal camera is one candidate.

### Limits of Phase 16

- The look-alike model is fitted on {{RESULT:multi_drone/clutter_p10_v2_cal.json#by_min_track_frames.1.recurrence.1.85814.later_passes|,d}} later passes over false-alarm sites in rendered towns; its share and firing rate have wide intervals.
- Passes over the person are independent, as in Phase 14. The measured correlation (a person seen on one pass is seen on the next {{RESULT:multi_drone/clutter_p10_v2_cal.json#by_min_track_frames.1.pass_correlation.after_detected.pd|.0%}} of the time, against {{RESULT:multi_drone/clutter_p10_v2_cal.json#by_min_track_frames.1.pass_correlation.after_missed.pd|.0%}} after a miss) would favour confirming, so the confirmed times are, if anything, cautious.
- A crew checking an alarm takes no time in the simulation; alarms are counted instead.
- Packet loss and a byte budget together were not tested. Under heavy loss compact sharing sends more repair messages, so a small budget may then slow it.
- The rest of Phase 14's limits apply: flat open ground, one standing person, no wind or battery limit.
