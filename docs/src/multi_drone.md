# Multi-drone search simulation (Phase 14)

Several drones search an area for one missing person. Each keeps its own map of where the person may be, flies where that map says a look is worth most, and shares what it has looked at with the others over an unreliable radio. The question (hypothesis H12): does sharing observations find the person sooner and cover less ground twice than drones searching on their own, and does it still help when radio packets are lost?

The camera in the simulation is not assumed. Its chance of seeing a person, and how often it raises a false alarm, are measured from the real pipeline's output on SynDrone (Phase 10), at the same heights, camera angles and speed. Design and every choice: D-752 in [decisions.md](decisions.md).

python scripts/multidrone_sensor_fit.py --name p10_v2_cal --split test --allow-test   # camera from Phase 10
python scripts/multidrone_sensor_fit.py --name v2_dfine_cal_rural --split development # rural Town07
python scripts/multidrone_run.py design --sensor results/multi_drone/sensor_p10_v2_cal.json
python scripts/multidrone_run.py test --sensor results/multi_drone/sensor_p10_v2_cal.json  # once, after freezing

## The world

- **Where the person is likely to be.** Lost hikers in temperate mountains: a quarter are found within 1.1 km of where they were last seen, half within 3.1 km, three quarters within 5.8 km and 95 % within 18.3 km (Koester, 2010; 568 cases). Each ring's share is spread evenly over it, as in the ring model studied by Sava et al. (2016). The search area is the square around the last-seen point reaching the first ring (4.8 km2). A flat prior is also run, to check that results do not depend on this choice.
- **Ground.** Flat and open. Forest, which hides people from a colour camera, is not modelled.
- **One person, standing still.**

## The drones and their camera

- **Flight.** SynDrone's three camera set-ups (20 m looking 30 degrees down, 50 m at 60 degrees, 80 m straight down) at 21.9 m/s, the speed of the SynDrone flights. Only ground at least 10 degrees below the horizon counts as searched.
- **Seeing the person.** Measured per pass (a stretch of time a person stays in view): the share of passes in which the tracker matched them. It rises steeply with how long they stay in view.
- **False alarms.** Measured per km2 of ground searched.
- **Two measured cameras.** The Phase 10 test towns (mostly streets) and Town07, the most rural SynDrone town.
- **Output.** Detections leave the camera model as the ROS 2 node's `TrackedObjectArray` messages and are read back by the same code the map uses.

## The search map and sharing

Each drone's map holds, for every 10 m cell, the probability that the person is there. A pass that sees nothing lowers a cell by the chance it would have seen someone; a detection raises its cell, by less where false alarms are common (Bayesian search, Koopman 1946; Stone 1975). Drones share their observations (which cells they passed, how well, and what they detected), not their maps. Each observation has a unique number, so a drone that receives one twice, or receives them in a different order, ends with the same map. Observations are passed on from drone to drone, so they reach drones out of each other's radio range.

The radio reaches drones within a set range, loses each packet with a set probability, and allows a set number of bytes per second. All three are swept rather than fixed at one guessed value.

## Planners

- **Lawnmower.** Each drone sweeps its own strip in parallel lanes, again and again. Needs no radio.
- **Greedy.** Each drone flies to the block of ground where it expects to find the person fastest, by its own map.
- **Auction.** As greedy, but each drone announces its choice; a drone that hears a better claim for its block chooses again.

The planner and its block size are chosen on development runs (seeds 0 to 199) by a rule fixed beforehand, then frozen; the comparison is run once on separate test seeds (10000 to 10299).

- **Byte budget: not measured by the test.** A fault in the radio model, found after the test, meant drones under a budget sent their block claims but almost none of their observations (below).
- **The flat prior** changes little for the lawnmower ({{RESULT:multi_drone/test_p10_v2_cal.json#results.lawnmower_uniform.restricted_mean_time_s|,.0f}} s) and slows the auction ({{RESULT:multi_drone/test_p10_v2_cal.json#results.chosen_share_uniform.restricted_mean_time_s|,.0f}} s): the ring prior's shape was helping the map-guided drones a little.

## What this means

**H12 (sharing observations reduces time to find and repeated coverage compared with independent search, and degrades gracefully with packet loss): partly supported.** Against the same planner without sharing, all three parts hold: sharing finds the person sooner at every team size and with both cameras, repeats less ground (slightly), and falls back no further than not sharing as packets are lost. It does not degrade gracefully when bandwidth is short: below the bytes it produces, its advantage soon disappears (development seeds, above). Against the other baseline, independent lawnmower strips, it does not: the lawnmower is faster at every team size, because the present detector's false alarms pull map-guided drones away from new ground. Map-guided search pays off only once false alarms are rare, or checked some other way before the map takes them in (development check: {{RESULT:multi_drone/design_check_p10_v2_cal.json#auction_b4_faoff_pdbylen.rmt|,.0f}} s against {{RESULT:multi_drone/design_check_p10_v2_cal.json#lawnmower_faoff_pdbylen.rmt|,.0f}} s without them). Until then, the recommended plan for a team is the lawnmower, one strip per drone.

## Limits

- One standing person, flat open ground, instantaneous turns, no wind, no battery limit.
- A crew checking detections, and the time that costs, is not modelled; false alarms are counted instead.
- Passes over the same person are treated as independent. Real repeat looks at the same spot are probably correlated, which flatters every planner that looks again.
- The camera was measured in rendered towns, mostly urban.
- The radio is a simple disc with independent losses.
