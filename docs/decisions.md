## Phase 1 (geometry core)

Every convention below is pinned by a test. The test names are given so a reviewer can check each one.

**D-101 Frames.** World is North, East, Down (NED); body is forward, right, down (FRD); camera follows OpenCV (x right, y down, z forward). `R_ab` maps frame b into frame a (`v_a = R_ab v_b`). Tests: `tests/test_frames.py`, `tests/test_camera.py`.

**D-102 Attitude.** Aerospace yaw-pitch-roll, `R_wb = Rz(yaw) Ry(pitch) Rx(roll)`. Positive pitch is nose up, positive roll is right wing down, and yaw is the heading clockwise from North (`test_ypr_sequence_is_yaw_then_pitch_then_roll`, `test_positive_pitch_is_nose_up`, `test_positive_roll_is_right_wing_down`, `test_yaw_90_points_body_forward_east`). The check against AU-AIR's body-frame figure is **not done yet**, because the paper had not been read yet. It happens in Phase 4, before any AU-AIR result. The paper's sample attitude (roll 0.011, pitch 0, yaw 1.26 rad) is used only as a heading round-trip test here.

**D-103 Camera mount and tilt sign.** Default mount `R_BC_FORWARD`: camera z to body forward, x to right, y to down. Tilt `δ` (depression) is applied as `R_bc = Ry(−δ) R_BC_FORWARD`. The minus sign follows from positive rotation about body right being nose up. The tests decide it: `δ = 0` puts the optical axis along body forward, and `δ = 90°` puts it along body down. One consequence is pinned by `test_nadir_mount_keeps_image_right_as_body_right_and_image_top_forward`: at nadir, the image top points to body forward and image right stays body right. The same rotation is used to perturb tilt in the uncertainty model.

**D-104 Quaternions.** Hamilton convention, active rotation, default component order `wxyz`, with `xyzw` available. Mid-Air's order and active/passive convention are still unknown. They are pinned in Phase 2 from the dataset's example scripts, with a known-orientation test.

**D-105 GPS to local NED.** Exact WGS 84 conversion through Earth-centred, Earth-fixed coordinates, instead of a tangent-plane approximation, so the approximation's validity never has to be argued. Constants: a = 6378137.0 m, f = 1/298.257223563 (NIMA TR8350.2, Table 3.1; EPSG:7030). Tests compare small offsets with the meridian and prime-vertical radii. They also check the second-order poleward term that a 6 m east offset produces (about E² tan φ / 2N, a few micrometres), which shows that the conversion is ellipsoidal rather than flat.

**D-106 Validity.** An estimate is invalid when the ray is at or above the horizon (Down component ≤ 0, reason `ray_at_or_above_horizon`), or when the camera is not above the plane (`camera_not_above_plane`). No numerical margin is added. Where the ray is valid but unusable (near the horizon), that is handled by the uncertainty model and the `MIN_DEPRESSION_DEG` reliability flag, which Phase 2 derives from data. The general plane adds `ray_parallel_to_plane` and `plane_behind_camera`.

**D-107 Height-aware contact point.** In `centre_with_height` mode, the ray is intersected with a plane raised by the object height. The returned point is the ground contact directly below that intersection, and `range_m` is measured to the ground contact, so both modes report the same physical quantity. `depression_deg` is the depression of the ray. Test: `test_centre_with_height_recovers_object_ground_position_at_nadir`.

**D-108 Lens distortion.** Brown-Conrady with five coefficients (k1, k2, p1, p2, k3, in OpenCV's order), implemented directly because OpenCV is not a dependency yet. Undistortion uses fixed-point iteration. The iteration cap and tolerance are numerical settings, checked by a round-trip test. With no coefficients, the pinhole model is assumed.

**D-109 First-order propagation.** Noise is modelled as perturbations about the nominal inputs. Attitude noise acts on the Euler angles (the form AU-AIR logs). Tilt noise is an extra rotation about body right. The Jacobian is of the 3D ground point, by central differences with step `ε^(1/3) · max(|θ_i|, σ_i)` (Nocedal and Wright, Numerical Optimization, 2nd edn). Then `Σ_P = J Σ_θ Jᵀ`, and range, ground-distance and bearing sigmas come from the gradients of those quantities with respect to P. Parameters with σ = 0 are skipped. Bearing sigma is undefined (NaN) for a point within rounding error of directly below. Correlated altitude and pitch are supported through a correlation map.

**D-110 Monte Carlo.** Draws use an eigen-decomposition square root, which handles singular (fully correlated) covariances. Invalid draws are dropped, counted and flagged (`mc_invalid_draws`). Range percentiles cover the 50, 68.27, 90, 95 and 99 per cent central intervals, and the azimuth spread uses wrapped differences. `nonlinearity_ratio` is the Monte Carlo range sigma divided by the first-order one. The plan said "disagree materially" but gave no threshold, so that threshold is a new placeholder, `NONLINEAR_RATIO_TOL` in `configs/uncertainty.yaml`, to be set in Phase 2. The number of draws, `N_MC`, must be passed explicitly; there is no default.

**D-111 Kalman filter.** State `[N, E, vN, vE]` in the world frame, with a constant-velocity model and continuous white-noise acceleration of spectral density `KALMAN_Q` (m²/s³). Two-point initialisation means no initial-velocity variance has to be invented. Updates use the Joseph form. Missed detections are predict-only. Heading is clockwise from North. Reference: Bar-Shalom, Li and Kirubarajan (2001), Estimation with Applications to Tracking and Navigation.

**D-112 Test tolerances.** The NEES test runs 200 seeded runs of 60 steps. It requires the run-averaged NEES to fall inside the two-sided 99 per cent chi-squared interval at no fewer than 96 per cent of time steps, and the time-averaged NEES to be within 10 per cent of 4. The first-order and Monte Carlo agreement test uses 20,000 draws and a tolerance of 5 per cent of the largest variance. All camera and noise values in the tests are synthetic fixtures. None of them is a dataset parameter.

**Reference check outstanding.** The section and table numbers cited in D-105, D-109 and D-111 are from memory. Verify them against the primary sources before they appear in the brief.

## Phase 2 (Mid-Air): unknowns resolved

Evidence: `results/midair/unknowns.json`, from `scripts/midair_unknowns.py` on Kite_training/sunny trajectories 0 to 6, every 100th frame. These are exploration settings, used only to choose frames for inspection. Sources were read on 2026-10-19: the Mid-Air technical specifications, data organisation and FAQ pages (https://midair.ulg.ac.be), and the example scripts `hdf5-depth_viewer.py` and `tools/IMU-data_generator.py` (github.com/montefiore-ai/midair-dataset). The regression tests in `tests/test_midair_real.py` keep these checks in the suite. They skip when the data is absent.

**D-201 Depth is radial.** The FAQ says depth is "the Euclidean distance of the objects to the focal point". The test agrees. Water (class 8) is genuinely flat, and plane fits to back-projected water pixels over 84 frames give a median RMS residual of 0.0067 m for radial depth against 2.13 m for planar. Radial wins in 96 % of frames. Depth decoding (PNG bits reinterpreted as float16, in metres) follows the example script.

**D-202 Quaternion convention.** Hamilton, `wxyz` (the HDF5 attribute `axis_ordering` says `wxyz`, and the loader refuses anything else). It is an active body-to-world rotation (`R_wb`), as in the IMU generator script. On real data, the IMU specific force predicted as `R_wbᵀ (a_w − g)`, with gravity along +Down, matches the accelerometer with RMS 0.05 to 0.20 m/s² per trajectory. The opposite convention gives 2.4 to 6.0 m/s². This also confirms that the world frame is NED. The gravity value of 9.81 m/s² is the one the dataset's own generator uses.

**D-203 Camera mount.** The FAQ gives camera-to-body as `(x_b, y_b, z_b) = (z_c, y_c, x_c)`, with pixel rows on `x` and columns on `y`. In OpenCV terms that is `R_BC_FORWARD` with tilt 0 and no lever arm (the HDF5 lists `local_position` (0, 0, 0) for the left camera). Test: the water-plane normal rotated into the world has a median tilt from vertical of 0.06° (95th percentile 0.16°), so no systematic mount offset shows at that level. The plan was to fit small offsets on the development split only if residuals are systematic. That check is repeated on the development split once the split exists. Not checked: the half-pixel convention, i.e. whether pixel centres sit at integer or integer + 0.5 coordinates. The site gives `cx = w/2`, which is used as given. At 1024 pixels with fx = 512, half a pixel is about 0.056°.

**D-204 Intrinsics.** fx = cx = w/2 and fy = cy = h/2 (technical specifications page), with no distortion (rendered images). Depth and segmentation are 1024×1024.

**D-205 Sky.** Sky pixels hold the largest float16 value (65504), not infinity. That value marks sky; it is not a real depth. Over 7 trajectories, the sentinel and segmentation class 0 (undocumented on the site) agree on all but 3,078 of 39.1 million sky pixels.

**D-206 Normals.** The normals are stored at 512×512, half the depth resolution. The decoding is the one on the specifications page: red → body Y, blue → body X, green → −body Z, each scaled from [−1, 1] to [0, 1]. Over 84 frames, the frame-mean water normal rotated into the world is within a median 0.49° of vertical, consistent with 8-bit quantisation around 127.5. Individual pixels scatter, with a median tilt of 2.95°. Consequence: slope must be estimated from normals averaged over a patch, not from single pixels, and the averaging window is a documented choice in the sampling step.

**D-207 Frame-to-ground-truth mapping.** Row = floor(100 k / 25) = 4k (data organisation page, example script). There are 4 × frames − 2 ground-truth rows (for example 2206 frames and 8822 rows), so every frame has a row.

**D-208 Mid-Air split.** By trajectory, seed 0, with 50 / 20 / 30 % for development, calibration and test (15 / 6 / 9 of the 30 Kite_training/sunny trajectories). Proposed and approved on 2026-10-19. The split is in `results/splits.json`, and `scripts/make_splits.py` refuses to overwrite it with a different split. **Disclosure:** the convention checks behind D-201 to D-207 were first run on trajectories 0 to 6, before the split existed. That set includes test trajectories 0001 and 0005 and calibration trajectory 0000. Those checks fitted and tuned nothing; they confirmed conventions already stated in the dataset documentation. They were then rerun on development trajectories only (0002, 0003, 0004, 0006, 0008), and `results/midair/unknowns.json` now holds that development-only run. Its numbers supersede those quoted in D-201 to D-206. Water-plane residual: median 0.0059 m radial against 2.48 m planar (radial better in 93 % of 45 frames). Water-plane tilt: median 0.061°. Frame-mean normal tilt: median 0.49°. Per-pixel normal scatter: median 3.06°. Quaternion residuals: 0.05 to 0.15 m/s² against 1.9 to 6.0 m/s² for the opposite convention. Sky and class 0 disagree on 187 of 28.6 million pixels. The real-data regression tests use development trajectory 0002.

**D-209 Pixel sampling.** Development values. They are revisited once all development trajectories are downloaded and the bins are populated.
- `frame_stride` = 25 (one frame per second); frames at 25 Hz are strongly correlated.
- `pix_per_frame` = 500 sampled pixels per frame, from ground classes 3, 4, 5, 10 and 11 plus water (8, reported separately).
- `depth_edge_rel` = 0.1 (new placeholder). A pixel counts as next to a discontinuity if a 4-neighbour's radial depth differs by more than 10 %. Measured on development frames: the histogram of neighbour jumps falls steadily and then flattens into a long tail near 10⁻¹. That tail is real edges (rocks, trees, terrain occlusion), and 0.1 sits above the 99th percentile of ground-to-ground jumps (0.053). Sky and non-ground classes also count as discontinuities.
- `edge_margin_px` = 3. Pixels within 3 px of a discontinuity or the image border are excluded, which clears anti-aliased edge pixels with margin. This is a judgement, not a measurement; a sensitivity check is planned.
- `normal_patch_px` = 9 (new placeholder). Slope comes from body normals averaged over a 9 × 9 patch of the half-resolution normals image, using sample-class pixels only, because single pixels scatter by about 3° (D-206). Sensitivity check planned.

**D-210 Oracle plane.** The oracle is a robust plane fitted per frame. Because the deployed pipeline uses a horizontal plane, `oracle_plane` uses a horizontal plane at the median Down coordinate of the frame's sampled true ground points. The median is robust to outliers. A tilted per-frame plane belongs to the slope experiment (H4), not to this mode. `relative_altitude` fixes the plane at the first usable frame's oracle height.

**D-211 Plane diagnostics for H4.** Two diagnostics are compared with the deployed flat oracle plane, binned by local slope. (a) `tilted_plane`: one robust plane per frame through the true ground points. It is fitted by least squares, residuals beyond 3 scaled MADs (1.4826 × MAD) are trimmed, and the fit is repeated up to three times. That is a standard robust trimming rule; it is not tuned. (b) `local_slope`: one plane per sample, using the sample's patch-averaged true normal, anchored at the frame medoid. The medoid is the sampled true ground point nearest the coordinate-wise median, so the anchor lies on the terrain; a plain median of 3D points need not. Both use true geometry, so they show how much of the error the flat-ground assumption explains. They are not deployable methods. Tests: exact on synthetic single-plane terrain.

**D-212 Mid-Air sensor-noise sweep (`SENSOR_NOISE`).** Sensor noise is swept and reported, not tuned. There are three levels (low, mid, high) in `configs/uncertainty.yaml`:
- pixel noise 0.5, 2 and 5 px;
- height noise 0.1, 0.5 and 2 m;
- roll, pitch and yaw noise 0.1°, 0.5° and 2° each;
- camera-tilt noise 0.1°, 0.5° and 2°;
- focal-length noise 0.25 %, 1 % and 4 %.

They span sub-degree to multi-degree attitude error. They are experimental design, not claims about any sensor. **No headline value is chosen here.** Data-driven altitude and attitude noise come from AU-AIR hover segments; until then, Mid-Air results are reported per sweep level. Pixel noise is per pixel; all other noise is drawn once per frame, because a sensor error is shared by every pixel in that frame.

**D-213 Bin reporting rule.** A bin is reported only with at least 100 samples (`MIN_SAMPLES_PER_BIN`) and samples from at least 3 trajectories (new placeholder `MIN_GROUPS_PER_BIN`). At 100 samples, a 90th percentile rests on 10 samples above it. Intervals resample whole trajectories, so fewer than 3 gives no meaningful interval. Unreported bins stay in the CSV tables with `reported = false`. Bin edges are listed in `scripts/midair_analyse.py`: finer near the horizon, and roughly logarithmic in range.

**D-214 Noise-induced error as the H2 test.** The Mid-Air error is terrain error plus sensor-noise error. To test the error-propagation predictions themselves, H2 compares the *noise-induced* change in range (noisy estimate minus noise-free estimate) with the first-order and Monte Carlo predicted medians (0.6745 σ, the median absolute value of a zero-mean Gaussian). Total error, with terrain included, is reported separately, along with the calibration of the predicted σ against it (H3).

## Phase 3 preparation (VisDrone)

**D-301 Matching threshold.** IoU ≥ 0.5 (`IOU_MATCH`), the MOTChallenge convention. The citation (Milan et al., 2016) is to be verified before the brief.

**D-302 Evaluation groups and ignored regions.** Person = pedestrian and people (1, 2); vehicle = car, van, truck and bus (4, 5, 6, 9); "others" (11) is excluded. Predictions whose box centre lies inside an ignored region (category 0) of that frame are removed before scoring. Ground truth is written per group in MOTChallenge format by `scripts/visdrone_prep.py --mot`. Counts per split are in `results/visdrone/prep.json`.

**D-303 Detector training labels.** Ten classes, categories 1 to 10, become YOLO class ids 0 to 9. That keeps the original classes, and they are mapped to the two groups at evaluation. Ignored regions and "others" are not written as labels. The image pixels inside ignored regions are left unchanged, so the detector may see unlabelled objects there as background. Masking those pixels is an alternative, considered for the fine-tuning ablation if it matters. Images are hard-linked, not copied. After removing ignored regions and "others", and after the leakage exclusion in D-308, the set has 6,414 train images (338,285 boxes) and 527 val images (37,135 boxes).

**D-304 Data layout.** I reorganised `Data/` into `VisDrone-MOT/` and `VisDrone-DET/` on 2026-10-19, and the config now has `visdrone_mot_subdir` and `visdrone_det_subdir`. `Data/annotations/` and `Data/images/` (1,610 files each) duplicate `VisDrone2019-DET-test-dev` by file name. They are unused and were left untouched until I decided. DET test-dev is not needed and is not used.

**D-305 Matplotlib.** Version 3.11.2, under the Matplotlib licence (BSD-style, based on the PSF licence). Figures use the validated reference palette's first three categorical slots, which pass the colour-vision checks across all pairs. Each figure has a CSV table beside it.

**D-215 `N_BOOT` = 1000 and `N_MC` = 1000.** These come from the stability check in `results/midair/development/stability.json`.
- Bootstrap: the interval endpoints of the oracle-plane median absolute relative error move by at most 1 % of the interval width between 1000 and 2000 resamples (3.5 % at 250).
- Monte Carlo: the per-sample range sigma changes by a median 1.8 % between 1000 and 2000 draws (95th percentile 6.1 %), consistent with the theoretical 1/√(2n).
- With only 6 development trajectories the intervals themselves are wide, so the remaining downloads matter more than more resamples.

**D-306 py-motmetrics with NumPy 2.** py-motmetrics 1.4.0 `distances.iou_matrix` calls `np.asfarray`, which NumPy 2 removed. The library is not patched. `aerialrange.track.evaluate.iou_distance` computes the IoU distance instead (tested), and only motmetrics' accumulator and metrics are used. Ultralytics installed `lap` 0.5.13, its tracker assignment solver, at run time; it is now pinned. Ultralytics also depends on the full `opencv-python`, which coexists with `opencv-python-headless` at the same version. Lockfiles: `requirements-lock.txt` holds the core, analysis and dev closure that CI installs (no torch); `requirements-lock.txt` holds the full environment, with the PyTorch CPU index.

**D-307 Detector family, sizes and input sizes.** YOLO26 (`DETECTOR_FAMILY`) is the newest family documented in the installed Ultralytics 8.4.182, which also ships a P2 small-object variant (`yolo26-p2.yaml`). Sizes n and s (`DETECTOR_SIZES`), for the CPU budget. Input sizes 640, 960 and 1280 (`INPUT_SIZES`): the library default, a midpoint, and close to native MOT val width (1344 px). Trackers are the library's ByteTrack and BoT-SORT with their default parameters; tuning happens later on validation only (`TRACKER_GRID`). BoT-SORT has re-identification off by default (`with_reid: False`). Camera-motion compensation is compared as BoT-SORT `gmc_method: sparseOptFlow` (`configs/trackers/botsort_cmc.yaml`) against `none` (`botsort_nocmc.yaml`); ByteTrack has no compensation. Ultralytics is AGPL-3.0, consistent with this repository's licence (D-005).

**D-308 VisDrone DET and MOT overlap: the papers' claim does not hold.** Results are in `results/visdrone/overlap.json`, from `scripts/visdrone_overlap.py`.
- **Provenance.** Both naming schemes carry the source video number (DET `0000201_00182_d_…`, MOT `uav0000201_00000_v`). By that number, 57 DET-train images (videos 137, 182, 201, 297, 305 and 339) and 21 DET-val images (videos 86, 117 and 249) come from videos that also supply MOT val or test-dev clips. A further 448 train and 106 val DET images share videos with MOT train, which is harmless for MOT evaluation.
- **Content.** 64-bit average hashes of every DET image against every 5th MOT frame give 610 matches within 4 bits, but only 157 of them come from the same video. The hash alone is therefore unreliable. One same-video pair was inspected by eye (DET `0000201_00182_d_0000172` against test-dev `uav0000201_00000_v` frame 91): it is the same scene a moment apart. One different-video pair was also inspected (DET-val `0000276_02001_d_0000517` against MOT train `uav0000239_12336_v` frame 46): different places that share only a sky-over-road layout.
- **Action.** The 57 + 21 images are excluded from detector training and model selection, which is 1.1 % of DET train and val. MOT val and test-dev therefore share no source video with the detector's training or selection data. DET test-dev is not used anywhere.

**D-309 Detection AP.** Our own implementation (`aerialrange.detect.evaluate`, tested): COCO-style greedy matching and 101-point interpolated AP, per evaluation group, with size bins on box height. Out-of-bin ground truth and unmatched out-of-bin detections are ignored, as COCO does for area ranges. It is needed because the zero-shot COCO model (B0) and the fine-tuned VisDrone model (B2) have different class sets, so both are scored on the person and vehicle groups.

**D-310 Tiled inference (B3).** Square tiles with fractional overlap; the last tile in each row and column is aligned to the image edge; class-wise NMS merges duplicates. Tile size and overlap are new placeholders (`TILE_SIZE`, `TILE_OVERLAP`), chosen on validation when B3 runs.

**D-311 Flag operating point and usable limits.** The flag threshold `REL_UNC_THRESHOLD` is chosen on the calibration split by maximum F1. That rule has no free parameter, which a target-recall rule would. `MIN_DEPRESSION_DEG` and `MAX_RANGE_M` use the same rule: the edge where the upper 95 % bound of the binned median absolute relative error (noisy mode, development split) reaches `ERR_THRESHOLD_FRAC`. One definition of "large error" therefore drives the flag, the limits and the precision/recall labels. `ERR_THRESHOLD_FRAC` is chosen from the sweep 0.05, 0.1, 0.2 and 0.5 (set to 0.1 in D-314). Script: `scripts/midair_calibrate.py`.

**D-312 Detection size bins.** Box-height bins of 0–16, 16–32, 32–64, 64–128 and 128+ px. Each bin doubles the previous one; small objects dominate VisDrone, so AP is reported by size. Detection AP on MOT frames uses every 10th frame of each sequence, because neighbouring frames are near-duplicates (`scripts/detect_eval.py`).

**D-313 Edge tooling.** onnx 1.23.2 (Apache-2.0), onnxruntime 1.31.0 (MIT) and onnxslim 0.1.98 (MIT), pinned in `requirements-lock.txt`. `scripts/export_onnx.py` always reports the actual maximum box and score differences and the detection agreement on a seeded sample of MOT val frames. It compares against `PARITY_TOL` only once that value is set with a justification.

## Author decisions, 2026-10-19

**D-314** (each item below is my decision):

- (b) Detector fine-tuning (B2) runs on a cloud GPU: free Kaggle first, paid if needed. The package is uploaded manually as a private dataset (`docs/gpu_training.md`, `gpu/train_b2_kaggle.py`). The package is `cache/gpu_package/visdrone_yolo.zip`, with its SHA-256 recorded in `manifest.json`.
- (c) The D-308 exclusion of 78 overlapping DET images is approved.
- (d) `MOT17_REPO_PATH` is https://github.com/LukeA77/MOT_Tracking at commit 455e702. Its `outputs/metrics.json` is copied to `results/mot17_reference/metrics.json` with provenance, for the domain-gap comparison. The repository holds no weights, so B1 needs my trained `.pt` file if he still has it.
- (e) `Data/annotations/` and `Data/images/` were verified as byte-identical copies of DET test-dev (all 3,220 files), and were deleted.
- (f) The uncertainty-model problem (H3) is **not** to be reported as a dead end. Find a solution; options are under discussion.

**D-315 Training schedule for B2 (`TRAIN_HYPERPARAMS`).** The library defaults (100 epochs, early-stopping patience 100) are changed to at most 50 epochs with patience 10, plus `cache="ram"`. Reasons:
- Fine-tuning from pretrained weights converges well before 100 epochs.
- The GPU quota is 30 hours a week.
- Early stopping uses DET val, which is not a test split.
- The earlier MOT17 run used 30 epochs.
- RAM caching only removes JPEG-decoding stalls; it does not change what is learned.

The first Kaggle attempt took about 1 hour per epoch, the same as the local CPU probe, which suggests the GPU was not active. The script now refuses to run without a GPU. Everything else stays at library defaults.

## Terrain-aware ranging (plan D + E + B + C, 2026-10-19)

This goes beyond the flat-ground plan, at my request (D-314 f). The flat-ground method stays as the baseline, so that "flat versus terrain-aware" is reported side by side.

**D-401 Terrain from motion (D).**
- Corners in frame k (Shi-Tomasi) are tracked to earlier frames k − g (pyramidal Lucas-Kanade, kept only if the forward-backward error is below 1 px).
- Each track is triangulated with the two known camera poses (midpoint method, `geometry/triangulation.py`, test-first, matching the stereo depth-sigma oracle).
- **Per-feature baseline choice.** Every corner is triangulated against each gap in (1, 2, 5, 10), and the estimate with the smallest total position variance (trace of the covariance) is kept. Exploration on development trajectory 0002 showed a single 5-frame gap loses near ground: fast image motion breaks tracking, the support radius grows to about 8 m, and steep views reach 8 % error, against 2 % with gaps of 1–2. Distant ground prefers longer gaps. Selecting by Down variance alone was rejected, because shallow rays hide their error along the nearly horizontal line of sight.
- Each target ray is intersected with a robust local plane height field fitted to its k = 30 nearest triangulated points (`geometry/terrain.py`). The neighbourhood size adapts to point density instead of using a fixed radius.
- The method uses only images and poses: no segmentation, depth or height above ground.
- Detector and tracker settings are common OpenCV values, not tuned (`configs/geometry.yaml`, `terrain_motion`).

**D-402 Tracking noise (`TERRAIN_SIGMA_PX`).** Measured on the development split (`scripts/measure_tracking_noise.py` → `results/midair/development/tracking_noise.json`). For each corner, the true 3D point (from radial depth, at locally smooth depth only) is projected into the earlier frame and compared with the tracked position. The robust sigma (mean of u and v, from 1.4826 × MAD) is used per gap. The tracker has a small core error with a heavy tail (90th-percentile errors are several times the core sigma). The core sigma drives the triangulation covariance; the tail is left to the conformal calibration (D-403), not hidden in a guessed number.

**D-403 Calibrated intervals (B).** Split conformal prediction (Vovk, Gammerman and Shafer, 2005; Lei et al., 2018), in `evaluate/conformal.py`. The score is |error| / sigma. The multiplier for level p is the ceil((n + 1) p)-th smallest calibration score, globally and per viewing-angle band ("Mondrian" conformal, with a global fallback for small bands). It is fitted on the calibration split and applied once to the test split. **Caveat:** samples within a trajectory are correlated, so the exchangeability guarantee is weaker than for independent samples. Coverage is therefore verified empirically on the test split, never assumed. References to be verified before the brief.

**D-404 Reliability flag (E).** L2-regularised logistic regression on a few interpretable per-estimate features (`evaluate/flag.py`), fitted on the calibration split only, with its coefficients reported. The L2 weight is a new placeholder (`FLAG_L2`), set before seeing test data.

**C (car-size cross-check)** is not built yet. Mid-Air has no vehicles, so it can only be evaluated on real footage (AU-AIR, not yet available).

**D-316 Detection cap (`max_det` = 1000).** The Kaggle training log reported VisDrone DET images with up to 902 objects (train) and 317 (val). The library default cap of 300 per image would cut recall in crowded frames, so every local inference (detection AP, tracking, pixel-noise fit) now uses 1000, set in `configs/detector.yaml`. B0 and B2 were re-scored with it, so they are compared under the same cap. B0 scores did not change: the zero-shot model never reached 300 detections.

**D-405 Image-space support for the terrain fit.** The development run with world-space k-nearest support (15 trajectories) showed a large-error tail: about 13 % of valid land estimates more than 50 % off. Almost all were large underestimates (median estimate/truth about 0.12), with support radius about 1.6 times the estimated range against 0.2 for good estimates. The iteration had locked onto a near, steep patch that the ray meets too early. Alternatives were compared on development trajectories 0003, 0016 and 0029, using every 4th evaluated frame (`cache/logs/neighbours_compare*.log`):
- **world** (k nearest in 3D around the ray): share of estimates more than 50 % off was 39 % / 3 % / 34 %, median 8.7 % / 1.2 % / 18 %.
- **image** (the k triangulated features nearest the target pixel, i.e. the patch the camera sees around the target): 13 % / 4 % / 14 %, median 6.1 % / 1.5 % / 5.6 %.
- **image then world** (image patch, then a world refinement): 9 % / 4 % / 19 %, median 4.7 % / 1.3 % / 11 %.

Image support is the most consistent: no trajectory fails badly. It is now the setting (`neighbours: image`). The refinement sometimes returns to the wrong patch and is not used. The full development rerun with image support replaces the earlier world-support numbers.

**D-317 Tiling with a full-frame pass (B3).** The first tile-only setting on MOT val (tile 480, overlap 0.2) raised small-object AP50 (vehicle 16–32 px: 0.345 against 0.266) but lowered overall AP50 (vehicle 0.477 against 0.587), because objects larger than a tile are split across tiles. `detect_tiled(..., include_full=True)` also detects the whole frame and merges it with the tiles through class-wise NMS, the usual remedy in sliced inference. It is tested with an object that no tile contains. Tile size and overlap are chosen on validation only, from the sweep in `cache/logs/overnight.log`.

**D-318 Tile merging by intersection over smaller area.** Tiled tracking (B3) on val was worse than untiled tracking (IDF1 vehicle 0.425 against 0.586), with precision falling from 0.69 to 0.52 and identity switches doubling. The cause: fragments of an object cut at a tile edge overlap the full detection with low IoU, so plain NMS keeps them. `nms(..., ios_thr)` also suppresses a lower-scoring box when at least `ios_thr` of the smaller box lies inside the kept box (intersection over smaller area, the merge criterion used by sliced-inference tools such as SAHI). It is tested on a tile-edge fragment and on adjacent separate objects. The threshold is chosen on val from {0.5, 0.8} (new placeholder `TILE_IOS`).

**D-319 Tracker grid from cached detections (`TRACKER_GRID`).** Written 2026-10-20, before any grid result was seen.
- *Method.* Each detector configuration runs once over MOT val, and every frame's boxes are saved (`scripts/cache_detections.py`, `track/detcache.py`). The tracker grid replays them (`scripts/track_grid.py`). Tracker settings do not change detection, so this gives the same tracks as live tracking at a small fraction of the cost. A test pins replay against live detection, and the B2 default grid point must reproduce the live B2 result (`B2_yolo26n_640_botsort_cmc.json`).
- *Score floor 0.1.* `model.track` asks the detector for boxes down to 0.1 (Ultralytics 8.4.182, `Model.track`: "trackers need low-confidence input"); the cache uses the same floor. **Finding:** the earlier tiled tracking runs (B3, `track_eval.py --tiled`) called `model.predict` at its default 0.25, so the tracker's second, low-score matching stage (`track_low_thresh` 0.1) never received boxes between 0.1 and 0.25. That made B3 against B2 an unequal comparison. The cached B3 run uses the 0.1 floor. Detection AP (`detect_eval.py`) keeps 0.25 for every model, so AP stays comparable across B0 to B3. Because the floor truncates the precision-recall curve, AP values sit below those from the usual 0.001 floor; this is noted, not changed mid-comparison.
- *Grid.* The two confidence thresholds of the library trackers, around their defaults (0.25, 0.25): `track_high_thresh` in {0.15, 0.25, 0.35, 0.45, 0.55} and `new_track_thresh` in {0.25, 0.35, 0.45, 0.55, 0.65}, with new ≥ high (15 points). `track_low_thresh` stays at 0.1 (the floor). Other settings stay at library defaults. Run for BoT-SORT with camera-motion compensation and for ByteTrack, on each detector: B2 n 640, B2 n 960, B2 s 640, and B3 (n 640, tiled, IoS 0.5).
- *Selection rule.* Highest mean IDF1 over person and vehicle (rounded to 4 decimal places), with ties broken by mean MOTA. Validation only; test-dev is not touched.

**D-320 Grid extension at the edge.** Added 2026-10-20, **after** the main grids had been seen. For both new detectors the selected BoT-SORT point lay on the grid boundary: n 960 at `track_high_thresh` 0.15 (lowest value tried) and s 640 at `new_track_thresh` 0.65 (highest value tried). A boundary optimum means the grid may have stopped short, so the grid is extended for these two (BoT-SORT with compensation only): high 0.05 with new 0.25 to 0.85, and high 0.15 to 0.55 with new 0.75 and 0.85 (`grid_*__ext_low.json`, `grid_*__ext_new.json`). The extension was added after seeing results, but it only widens the search; it does not change the rule.

**D-321 Tuned tracker per detector (`TRACKER_GRID` resolved).** From `results/visdrone/val/grid_summary.json` (MOT val, development; 22 to 32 points per detector and tracker).

**D-510 Stationary targets (`STATIONARY_PX_THRESHOLD`, `STATIONARY_MIN_S`).** `evaluate/stationary.py` (test-first, 5 tests) and `scripts/auair_stationary.py`. Camera motion between consecutive frames is a homography fitted to background corners with all tracked boxes masked out; a vehicle's residual step is its box-centre position minus where the background motion carried it. Steps are summed as **vectors**, so a parked car's jitter cancels while creeping adds up (summing step sizes would make jitter look like motion). A segment is stationary while the accumulated residual stays within the threshold of its start.
- Threshold per track: 3 × √2 × σ_pos, with σ_pos² = (κ × box size)² + σ_H². κ is the detector's measured vehicle box noise (larger of κ_u and κ_v, `results/visdrone/val/pixel_noise_B2_yolo26n_960.json`, about 0.053; measured on VisDrone, so applying it to AU-AIR is an assumption), and σ_H is the clip's homography noise (robust spread of RANSAC inlier residuals). √2 because the start-to-end residual of a parked object is the difference of two noisy positions; 3 for a three-sigma bound. For a 100 px car this is about 23 px, while a car moving 1 m/s at 20 m height moves about 12 px per frame at 5 fps.
- Minimum duration 4 s (20 frames at 5 fps), enough frames for a stable spread estimate per segment.
- No altitude or GPS is read, so repeatability is not circular.

**D-610 Detection stride and an overlapped pipeline (chosen 2026-10-22).** Options considered after the first Pareto study (best 13.3 fps): skip frames and overlap stages; smaller inputs only; stop and report. Chosen: skip frames and overlap.
- *Detection stride N:* the detector runs on every N-th frame. In between, the tracker is not updated, because an update without detections deletes new, unconfirmed tracks in the library; it coasts instead (`track.runner.coast`: Kalman predict of tracked and lost tracks, then BoT-SORT's camera-motion warp, mirroring the library's update up to matching), and the predicted boxes of confirmed tracks are output. Tested with ByteTrack and BoT-SORT: one identity, a box on every frame, predicted boxes between their detected neighbours; replayed detections stay in step (`Replay.skip`).
- *Overlap:* `scripts/benchmark_threaded.py` reads and detects frame k+1 on a second thread while the main thread tracks frame k (queue of 2). It reports throughput (frames per second the pipeline sustains) and latency (start of reading to tracker output, including the queue), with verdicts for both. ONNX Runtime and OpenCV release Python's global lock while computing.
- Accuracy of each stride on MOT val with the tuned thresholds of B2 n 960 (not re-tuned per option); latency on the quiet laptop (D-601).

**D-701 SynDrone town-level split (2026-11-04).** Written to `results/splits.json` (key `syndrone`) before any SynDrone frame was evaluated. Options considered: (a) the official split, (b) a seeded random split of whole towns, (c) a seeded split of whole towns stratified by town type. (a) is not by town: its test set is frames 0-99, 600-699, 1200-1299, 1800-1899 and 2400-2499 of every town (`splits.zip`; paper Section 3.3), so the same streets, parked cars and pedestrians appear in training and test; only a split by town keeps scenes out of both. (b) with 8 towns can put every city, or the only rural town, into one split, and the test result would then describe one kind of scene. (c) is chosen: three strata from CARLA's own map descriptions (CARLA 0.9.12 documentation, maps page): small towns (Town01, Town02), cities (Town03, Town05, Town10HD), highway and rural (Town04, Town06, Town07); one town per stratum to development and one to test, the remaining two to calibration, shuffled within each stratum with the project seed (0) by `data.splits.split_stratified` (tested). Bootstrap intervals resample whole sequences; the three cameras of one town share the same objects, so intervals that resample towns are also reported where they differ, because they are the more honest unit. **D-702 SynDrone storage: stream, do not store, what is used once (2026-11-04).** One town is about 10 GB with depth (all eight about 90 GB) and only about 10 GB is usable above the 15 GB floor until an external SSD is available. Options: (a) wait for the SSD; (b) download and delete one town at a time; (c) read single archive members over HTTP byte ranges and keep only what is reused. **D-703 SynDrone speed truth (2026-11-04).** Measured on Town01 (development): stationary actors do not move at all (position spread about 1e-12 m); moving actors' position jitter, measured from third differences while moving, is 0.015 mm median, 0.05 mm at the 90th percentile. So the smoothing width is set by bias, not noise: the shortest centred window, a 3-frame central difference (exact for constant acceleration), giving a speed uncertainty from jitter of about 0.001 m/s; the change from using 5 frames is 0.0002 m/s median and 0.24 m/s at the 99th percentile (an upper bound on the bias from acceleration changes), both reported in `results/syndrone/development/speed_truth_Town01.json`. Jumps: walkers are spawned with a maximum speed of 1 to 2 m/s (`spawn_utils.py`); their frame-to-frame speeds have a shoulder to 2.5 m/s and fall about 40-fold above it, with steps up to 100 m/s from a few walkers (one walker has 75 such steps), which are physics glitches, so walker tracks are cut above 2.5 m/s. Vehicles are cut above 70 m/s (none above 17.6 m/s in Town01). No derivative crosses a cut; 0.2 % of actor-frames have no speed as a result. Heading is shown only above 0.5 m/s. Speed truth is horizontal (x, y); the vertical component is not used.

**D-704 SynDrone truth per object (2026-11-04).** (1) *Base height z0:* SynDrone stores the camera height above an unpublished per-frame base height (the "floor height" of a recorded flight log, `data_handling.py`). Options: depth at the image centre alone (needs the ground height there, unknown in hilly towns), a single-pixel shortcut, or a joint fit. Chosen: vehicles stand on the road, so the road pixels in a 1.5 m ring around each vehicle's footprint (road and road-line classes only, so kerbs cannot bias it; at least 30 pixels) must lie at the box's bottom; each vehicle gives a median offset, and a Huber M-estimate over all vehicles from all three cameras of the frame gives z0 and its standard error (`evaluate/syndrone_truth.py`, tested on rendered synthetic scenes to 2 cm). The three cameras are independent views of the same unknown, so their agreement is a check, not an assumption. (2) *Instance masks without instance labels:* pixels of the right class whose 3D position from depth falls inside the box footprint (0.3 m margin); horizontal position from depth does not depend on z0, so the masks are independent of the z0 they check. (3) *Visibility:* among pixels whose ray passes through the 3D box, object pixels over object plus occluder pixels (an occluder is anything more than 0.3 m nearer than the box entry). (4) *2D truth box:* the box around the instance mask (the visible extent), not the projected 3D box: a 3D box's projection includes empty corners, most at oblique views, and detectors are trained on visible extents (VisDrone). Both are stored. (5) *Classes:* person = walkers (tag 40); vehicle = car, truck, bus (100-102), as the version 1 VisDrone groups; riders, two-wheelers and trams/trains, map ("level") boxes, and person or vehicle actors below the visibility or size thresholds are "ignore": a prediction matching one is neither true nor false positive. Map boxes are never tracking truth: 15 of Town01's 40 duplicate a spawned actor. (6) *Ignore matching:* TrackEval's MOT rule (Hungarian match of predictions against valid and ignored truth at IoU 0.5; predictions matched to ignored truth removed) rather than version 1's centre-in-region rule, because SynDrone objects overlap (a bicycle beside a car), where a centre rule removes the wrong prediction. (7) *Range truth:* slant distance from the camera to the footprint centre; the near-edge point (closest footprint point to the camera's ground position) is kept as the Phase 9 diagnostic.

**D-705 Which SynDrone objects count as truth (2026-11-05).** The visibility threshold is set on development towns. On Town01 (every third chunk, 41,240 person and vehicle views with any visible pixel), 77 % have visibility 0.9 to 1.0 and the rest are spread almost evenly between 0 and 0.9, with no valley to put a threshold in. 0.5 is used: most of the object is visible, and because the tail is flat, moving the threshold by 0.1 changes about 2 % of objects. Objects below it, and person or vehicle views with fewer than 4 mask pixels, are ignored, not missed (D-704). Small objects are otherwise kept and reported by box-height bin (half of all views have fewer than 68 mask pixels), as for VisDrone in version 1, so small-object failure stays visible rather than being filtered out.

**D-706 Reprojection threshold and z0 check, Town01 (2026-11-05).** The reprojection threshold is proposed from the data. The error measured is between the projected 3D box and the instance-mask box of clean views (person or vehicle actor, visibility at least 0.9, untruncated, at least 100 mask pixels). Part of it is not error at all: an object's shape is smaller than its box (left and top edges negative, right and bottom positive). At the straight-down camera a z0 error hardly moves the projection, so its median edge error, 3 px, measures that gap alone; `{{REPROJ_PX_MAX}}` = 3 + 1 px (rasterisation) = 4 px. Town01 result (`results/syndrone/development/z0_check_Town01.json`): median |edge error| 2 / 3 / 3 px at 20 / 50 / 80 m (all under 4), median |centre offset| 1.0 / 1.5 / 1.0 px; z0 standard error 5 mm median, 13 mm at the 90th percentile; per-camera z0 estimates agree within 0.6 to 2.6 cm; 83 % of frames fitted (at least 3 vehicles), the rest interpolated in time (the series is smooth: 0.3 mm median residual against 5-frame interpolation). Contact sheet `cache/syndrone/figures/reproj_Town01.jpg` (dataset imagery, not committed): boxes sit on the cars, the bus and the pedestrians at all three heights. The test is stricter in hilly towns (Town07), where z0 varies.

**D-707 SynDrone fine-tuning design (2026-11-05).** Detection is reported two ways: the VisDrone-trained detector with no SynDrone training, and the same fine-tuned on development towns. Scoring a model fine-tuned on all three development towns on those towns would be in-sample. Options: (a) score on calibration towns (they exist to fit calibration, and would be spent on detector scoring); (b) leave one development town out. (b) is chosen: three folds, each trained on two development towns and scored on the third, so every development town gets an out-of-town score and no other split is touched. Within a fold, early stopping uses the last 20 % of frames (24 s) of each training town, a contiguous block, so selection frames are separated in time from training frames. Every 5th frame (0.2 s apart) is used because 25 Hz neighbours are near-duplicates. Fine-tuning starts from the version 1 weights (`B2_yolo26n_960`, best.pt), with version 1's settings (D-315), so the only change is SynDrone training. Ignored objects (riders, two-wheelers, trams, map boxes, and views below the visibility rule) are filled with the image mean in the training images instead of being left unlabelled, because unlabelled real objects would be learnt as background; version 1 could leave VisDrone's ignored regions as they were because those are crowd or clutter regions, not single objects.

## Phase 8 (permissive detector and tracker)

**D-713 The project's own tracker (2026-11-06).** Version 1 tracked with Ultralytics' BoT-SORT, which is AGPL-3.0; the version 2 package must not depend on it. Options: (a) another permissive library tracker (for example a ByteTrack port); (b) our own implementation. (b) is chosen: the method is small, a dependency would still need checking line by line against version 1's behaviour, and our own code can be tested piece by piece. `track/tracker.py` (`MotionTracker`), `track/box_kalman.py` and `track/camera_motion.py` are written from the papers (ByteTrack, Zhang et al. 2022: two-stage association by score; BoT-SORT, Aharon et al. 2022: box-size Kalman state and camera-motion compensation; DeepSORT noise weights), with no library code; appearance features stay off. Choices where the papers leave room: (1) the camera motion is a similarity transform fitted by RANSAC to Lucas-Kanade flow of corners kept away from detected objects, the method version 1 already used on AU-AIR footage; (2) the transform moves box centres and velocities by its rotation and scale, and box sizes by its scale only (rotating a width into a height is not meaningful); (3) the gated assignment leaves out tracks and detections with no allowed partner, which gives the same answer (tested against the full problem) with a far smaller solve in crowded frames; (4) lost tracks age in frames, including frames without detection under a detection stride. Parity, on the same cached B2 YOLO26n 960 validation detections and the same scoring as the version 1 grid (`scripts/track_variants.py`, results/visdrone/val/track_variants_parity.json): with version 1's settings, mean IDF1 0.618 against version 1's 0.617 at camera-motion downscale 8 (the pipeline's setting, D-609) 0.619 against 0.619 at downscale 4 (version 1's figure from results/edge/pareto.json), and 0.616 against 0.620 at 2, where the flow window covers the least motion; without motion compensation 0.540. Speed: the optical flow was 85 % of the time, so five cheaper flow settings were scored, with the rule fixed beforehand: the fastest whose validation IDF1 stays at or above version 1's at the same downscale (0.6168). Chosen: no forward-backward check (RANSAC already rejects the outliers) and 400 corners at least 6 px apart, mean IDF1 0.618; smaller flow windows fell below the bar (0.613, 0.615) (results/visdrone/val/track_variants_cmc_speed.json). Single-threaded on this laptop, the tracker takes 17.5 ms per frame on the most crowded validation sequence (184 detections per frame) against 31.7 ms for version 1's, and about 7 ms against 15 ms on typical ones. Detection stride (D-610), same detections: mean IDF1 0.600 against version 1's 0.603 at every 2nd frame, 0.581 against 0.575 at every 3rd (results/visdrone/val/track_variants_stride.json). One difference is deliberate: lost tracks age in frames, where version 1 counted only detection frames, so its memory doubled in time at stride 2; matching that (60 or 90 frames) scores lower (0.596, 0.580), so it is not the cause, and the 0.003 gap at stride 2 is within the run-to-run variation. Settings: `configs/trackers/motion.yaml`, now used by `configs/run.yaml` and `configs/run_accurate.yaml`. Tested: `tests/test_motion_tracker.py`.

**D-714 No further tracker changes on this evidence (2026-11-06).** Three additions were scored one at a time against `configs/trackers/motion.yaml` (mean IDF1 0.6178; results/visdrone/val/track_variants_improve1.json): no matching across person and vehicle classes (identical result: no track ever switched group on validation); boxes enlarged by 10, 20 or 30 % before IoU (0.6176, 0.6139, 0.6157, with fewer person and more vehicle identity switches); lost tracks kept 60 frames (0.6172) or 15 (0.6181). **D-728 Motion model for speed: interacting multiple models with gating (2026-11-07).** Version 1 filters every object with one constant-velocity model, so parked objects show speeds made of measurement noise, turning vehicles lag, and a wrong association drags the estimate. Options: (a) keep constant velocity and tune its noise; (b) add a speed threshold below which nothing is shown; (c) an interacting multiple model filter (Blom and Bar-Shalom, 1988) with stationary, constant-velocity and coordinated-turn models, innovation gating, and a restart after repeated gate failures. (a) cannot serve parked and moving objects with one noise level; (b) hides the symptom without fixing the estimate. (c) is chosen, as the specification for this phase asks: `geometry/imm.py` (shared state north, east, velocity and turn rate; extended Kalman propagation for the turn model; Joseph-form updates; mode likelihoods in log space). Gating uses the chi-square quantile of the combined prediction's normalised innovation; after `reset_after` rejections in a row the track restarts from two new measurements, as a new track does, because the filter cannot know the new object's speed. No setting has a default: noise densities, switching matrix, gate probability and reset count are fitted on SynDrone development towns and checked on AU-AIR before use. Tests on synthetic tracks (`tests/test_imm.py`): the coordinated-turn step is exact on a circle and reduces to constant velocity at zero turn rate; its Jacobian matches finite differences; a parked object settles on the stationary model with under half the constant-velocity filter's noise speed; straight and turning motion select their models; the estimate is roughly consistent (mean NEES within a band around 4 over 40 runs); a gated-out measurement changes nothing. The pipeline uses it only when the run configuration names `motion_model: imm` (`IMMFilterBank`, same outputs plus the stationary probability); version 1's filter stays the default until the comparison on the same samples is done.

**D-734 Why AU-AIR vehicle tracks read implausibly fast: diagnosis (2026-11-08).** Version 1 found 10.6 % of AU-AIR vehicle tracks with a median speed above 70 km/h (D-518). AU-AIR has no true speeds, so each candidate cause was removed in turn and the speeds recomputed with everything else as in version 1 (`scripts/auair_speed_causes.py`, `results/auair/speed_causes_B2_yolo26n_960.json`; version 1's tracks, ground points, noise model and `kalman_q`; the unmodified variant reproduces version 1 exactly, 1,106 tracks, 10.58 %). *Identity switches are not the cause:* matched frame by frame to AU-AIR's own boxes linked into tracks, tracks with a switch read fast less often than tracks without (9.8 % against 13.1 %), and cutting tracks at switches raises the share (13.6 %, shorter pieces). The linked annotation boxes, with no detector or tracker at all, still give 9.0 %. *Frame timing (a version 1 evaluation error):* version 1 timed AU-AIR measurements as frame count × 0.2 s, but 4.3 % of AU-AIR frame steps are dropped frames (0.4 to 2 s); timestamps lower the share to 9.7 %. The pipeline already uses timestamps; only the AU-AIR evaluation script had the error. *Shallow lines of sight are the main cause:* by the track's median depression, 100 % of tracks below 3° read fast, 77 % at 3 to 5°, 47 % at 5 to 7.5°, 21 % at 7.5 to 10°, 9 % at 10 to 15°, 6 % at 15 to 20°, 1.6 % at 20 to 30° and none above 30° (ranges 130 to 530 m below 10°). *It is not white measurement noise:* each track replayed as a parked object with noise drawn from version 1's own noise model never exceeds 70 km/h (0 of 1,106; median 4 to 7 km/h below 7.5°). *It is slow, correlated pose and box error that version 1's noise model does not contain:* 7.6 % of tracks parked by image motion alone (D-510, no pose used) still read above 70 km/h. In those 25 tracks the computed range of a parked car swings by 6 m to several kilometres; in about half, range follows the per-frame tilt estimate (correlation −0.9 to −1.0; tilt varies 2 to 5° within the track as estimates enter and leave its 10 s window); in most others it follows the box's bottom edge, which moves 40 to 150 px (occlusion, truncation, box shape), and near the horizon both are magnified (range ∝ 1/tan depression). Holding the tilt at each track's median lowers the share to 7.7 % (7.2 % with timestamps). The drone's own motion does not explain it: it mostly hovers (median 0.03 to 0.09 m/s), and the share does not rise with its turn rate. *Consequence for the fix:* version 1's measurement noise is isotropic, relative to slant range within coarse depression bands, and contains neither tilt uncertainty nor the 1/sin² growth along the line of sight, so shallow measurements are trusted far too much and their errors pass into speed. The fix (D-735) propagates pixel, tilt and height uncertainty through the ray geometry into a full 2 × 2 measurement covariance, then applies the interacting multiple model filter of D-728 with gating and its stationary model, and reports speeds against version 1 on the same tracks.

**D-735 Speed fix: measurement covariance through the viewing geometry, and the IMM filter fitted to truth (2026-11-08).** From D-734. Method written before any IMM result was seen. *Measurement errors* (`geometry/ground_cov.py`, `kalman.meas_model: propagated`): the 2 × 2 covariance of each ground point by first-order propagation through the ray–ground intersection of independent errors in the contact pixel (σu = κu × box width, σv = κv × box height, κ per group from the detector's measured box noise on VisDrone validation, `scripts/fit_pixel_noise.py`), camera tilt, height and heading; the Jacobian is written in closed form (along the line of sight it grows as 1/down², the H2 effect) and tested against finite differences. **D-736 Range to the footprint centre, not the box's bottom edge (2026-11-08).** Written before any corrected result was seen. There is no threshold on when the heading is trusted. *Uncertainty:* the variance of the offset (size spread and heading, by the law of total variance) is added in quadrature to the range sigma. **D-741 Camera, IMU and GPS fusion: design and choices (Phase 11, 2026-11-09).** build2 Section 10 asks for a tightly coupled multi-state constraint Kalman filter (MSCKF; Mourikis and Roumeliotis, 2007) with IMU propagation at 100 Hz and online bias estimation, camera updates from tracked features, GPS position and velocity at 1 Hz, and consistency by first-estimate Jacobians, checked with NEES. Built in `src/aerialrange/navigation/` (tests `tests/test_navigation.py`: rotation maps, propagation against a continuous trajectory and its Jacobian against finite differences for body- and world-frame gyroscopes, projection Jacobians, alignment accuracy, and Monte Carlo NEES of the filter and of the alignment). Choices, each the most robust option found, with the evidence:

1. *Mid-Air's gyroscope is in the world frame.* The file labels ground-truth angular velocity and the gyroscope "body", but regressed on the attitude-derived rates the world-frame fit is the identity (residual 0.001 rad/s) and the body-frame fit is not (0.010 to 0.015 rad/s); integrating the noise-free ground-truth rates for 1 s from the true attitude gives a median error of 0.8 to 5.9 deg per flight as body rates (worst 47 deg) and 0.0003 to 0.0008 deg as world rates (all 15 development flights; `scripts/msckf_imu_frames.py`, `results/midair/development/navigation/imu_frames.json`). The IMU generator adds noise and bias to that vector, so they are world-frame too. The filter takes the gyroscope frame as a setting (`gyro_frame`: body for real hardware, world for Mid-Air) with the matching error-state model, rather than rotating the data with the true attitude, which would leak ground truth. The accelerometer is body-frame, as labelled (checked the same way). Version 1 used Mid-Air's poses only, not its IMU, so no version 1 result is affected. Consecutive IMU samples are averaged per 10 ms step (1 s open-loop attitude error, median over flights, 0.03 to 0.07 deg with each sample held, under 0.001 deg averaged).
2. *IMU noise is not recorded and differs per flight* (the generator draws it per flight within bounds spanning two orders of magnitude). Assuming the bound would make the filter needlessly vague on quiet IMUs and one typical value would be wrong on noisy ones; instead the white noise is estimated from the IMU signal itself (median absolute deviation of second differences, causal, refreshed at every GPS fix): within 0.98 to 1.05 of the ground-truth level over a flight, 0.82 to 1.22 from the first 3 s (`results/midair/development/navigation/imu_noise_check.json`). The bias random walk cannot be seen that way and is set to the generator's upper bound (conservative).
3. *GPS error model.* Mid-Air simulates a single-frequency receiver from pseudoranges without ionosphere correction, so errors are correlated. On the development flights (`gnss_fit.json`): a constant offset per flight (root mean square 0.92 / 0.64 / 12.5 m north / east / down; the down offset is about 12 m in every flight), a Gauss-Markov part (0.63 / 0.68 / 1.78 m, time constant 2.7 s) and white noise (0.35 / 0.29 / 0.97 m), all three kept as filter states or noise, with the offset's mean included in its variance so no dataset-specific mean is subtracted. Scaling by the recorded dilution of precision was tested and increased the spread between flights (coefficient of variation 0.51 against 0.40), so the model is in metres; build2 asked for DOP weighting, and this deviation is by the evidence.
4. *GPS velocity in Mid-Air is noise-free* (at most 7 mm/s from truth). Feeding it unchanged would give the filter a velocity no receiver provides, so white noise is added at the u-blox NEO-M8 data-sheet accuracy (UBX-13003366 R10, 0.05 m/s, read conservatively as a per-axis median: sigma 0.074 m/s), seeded per flight, labelled synthetic in every result.
5. *Start in flight.* Mid-Air flights start moving at 5 to 20 m/s and have no magnetometer, so there is no static levelling. Attitude and both IMU biases are fitted jointly (Gauss-Newton from a Wahba start) on 1 s GPS velocity differences against integrated specific force, with the full covariance of those differences (adjacent ones share a fix), the gyroscope's random walk along the attitude path, bias priors from the dataset's stored initial estimates (with the generator's stated error) plus the walk over the window, and a full-turn prior so an undetermined yaw comes out as unknown rather than known (a pseudo-inverse had hidden it). IMU and GPS alone cannot fix yaw in steady flight: flight 0026 started 61 deg off with a stated sigma of 33 deg, and the filter then locked onto the wrong yaw. The camera's direction of travel (essential matrix between frames 0.4 s apart; uncertainty from bootstrapping its inlier matches; accurate to 1 to 5 deg with a slightly conservative stated sigma on four development flights) against the GPS direction is added to the same fit, which fixes yaw without acceleration. The window grows from 2 s (OpenVINS's init window) until the attitude sigma is under 5 deg, at most 10 s.
6. *Settings from OpenVINS's published EuRoC configuration* where Mid-Air gives no reason to differ (11 clones, 40 features per update, 200 features, 10 px spacing, chi-squared gate at 95 %, first-estimate Jacobians on), with version 1's feature tracker (Shi-Tomasi, pyramidal Lucas-Kanade checked forward and backward) and a fundamental-matrix RANSAC.
7. *Feature noise.* With OpenVINS's 1 px the filter was overconfident over whole flights (attitude NEES 8 to 47 against 3; `msckf_dev1.json`) while the feature innovations were small (normalised innovation 0.2 per dimension): single feature errors are below 1 px (0.14 px against ground-truth poses), but errors are correlated along a track and between consecutive uses of the same scene point, which the MSCKF treats as independent. First-estimate Jacobians were not the cause (switching them off changed little). Rule written before the grid ran: the smallest sigma in {2, 3, 4} px whose pooled NEES over all nine states lies at or below the upper 95 % band, ties by relative pose error. Pooled NEES: 2 px 18.06, 3 px 11.55, 4 px 8.98 (band 6.98 to 11.27). The choice sat at the grid's edge with accuracy still improving, so the grid was extended upward (as D-320): 5 px 7.80, 6 px 7.22, with attitude underconfident at 6 px (1.68, below its band 1.89 to 4.36). **4 px is chosen by the rule**; 5 px also qualifies and is marginally more accurate (median tilt error 0.343 against 0.368 deg, 1-frame translation error 5.4 against 5.6 mm), which is reported, not acted on.

*Development result* (15 flights, `results/midair/development/navigation/msckf_sig4.json` against IMU and GPS only, `msckf_nocam.json`; medians over flights; GPS velocity noise synthetic as in 4). Camera fusion cuts the relative pose error at the triangulation baselines about six-fold: translation 5.6 mm against 34 mm over 0.04 s, 7.3 cm against 38 cm over 0.4 s; rotation 0.05 / 0.21 deg against 0.05 / 0.36 deg. **D-744 GPS outlier gate and reset (Phase 11 follow-up, 2026-11-09).** Open from D-741's test: GPS updates were not gated, and three consecutive fixes 40 to 100 m off on test flight 0014 were taken in. Options: (a) one chi-squared gate on the joint position-and-velocity innovation at the feature gate's 95 %; (b) PX4's estimator (EKF2, github.com/PX4/PX4-Autopilot/blob/main/src/modules/ekf2/EKF/common.h, read 2026-11-09): position and velocity gated separately, each axis against 5 standard deviations of its innovation (`ekf2_gps_p_gate`, `ekf2_gps_v_gate` = 5), and after 7 s with no accepted measurement the states are reset to the measurement (`reset_timeout_max`); (c) a robust (heavy-tailed) GPS likelihood. (b) is chosen: it is the default of a widely flown autopilot, so its levels are not tuned on these flights; gating the blocks separately keeps good velocity when only the position jumps; and the reset prevents the failure a gate alone has, a filter that has drifted and then rejects every fix for ever (without GPS ours is overconfident, D-741, so its covariance would not grow fast enough to readmit them). (a) rejects 5 % of good fixes by construction and lets one bad axis discard good ones; (c) changes the filter's model of every fix to handle rare ones. *Reset in a sliding-window filter:* the position innovation is applied as a common translation of the current pose and every clone (the camera constraints between clones are unchanged by a translation), with the innovation covariance added to all of them along that common translation; a velocity reset sets the velocity likewise, with the GPS velocity variance. *Check, written before any run* (`scripts/msckf_midair.py --gps-gate --inject`; the Mid-Air test flights are not used again): on the 15 development flights, (A) clean without the gate (`msckf_sig4`), (B) clean with it, (C) injected bursts without it, (D) injected bursts with it, (E) an injected step with it. Bursts: flight 0014's three-fix deviation pattern added at three seeded fix times per flight after the alignment window, at least 10 fixes apart; step: the burst's largest deviation added to every fix from a seeded time to the end (a persistent wrong offset, which only the reset can follow). Adopted if (1) in B all four pooled NEES are within their 95 % bands, (2) in D all four are within their bands and every injected fix is rejected, and (3) in E, on every flight, the filter accepts GPS again within 7 s plus one fix of the step and never again rejects for more than 7 s. B against A and C against D are reported in full. *Result* (`results/midair/development/navigation/gps_gate_check.json`; runs `gate_clean`, `nogate_burst`, `gate_burst`, `gate_step` against `msckf_sig4`; medians over flights, NEES pooled). (1) Clean flights with the gate: NEES 2.76 / 3.12 / 3.31 / 9.08 for attitude / position / velocity / all nine (without it 2.76 / 3.01 / 3.31 / 8.98; bands 1.89 to 4.36 and 6.98 to 11.27); relative pose error and tilt unchanged (5.6 mm over 0.04 s, 7.3 cm over 0.4 s, 0.37 deg); 6 of 1,279 good fixes rejected (0.5 %), 4 of them on flight 0008, the noisiest development GPS (deviations up to 11 m), and no reset. (2) Injected bursts: without the gate NEES 29.5 / 80.2 / 17.4 / 128.3, horizontal error 8.2 m, tilt 0.61 deg; with it 2.76 / 3.10 / 3.33 / 9.08, 0.85 m, 0.37 deg, the same as on clean flights, and all 135 injected fixes rejected. (3) Injected step: on every flight GPS position was used again by a reset 6 or 7 fixes after the step, with no later reset and no rejection longer than one fix afterwards. Its position NEES against the truth is large (4,776) by construction: after the step the GPS is wrong by 134 m, and the reset makes the filter follow it, which is the intended behaviour for an error that does not end (an on-board filter cannot tell a persistent receiver error from its own drift). **All three conditions hold, so the gate is adopted** (`configs/navigation.yaml`, `gps_gate.enabled: true`). The Mid-Air test result of D-741 is that of the frozen filter without the gate and is not re-scored; the test flights are not used again.

**D-745 Map output (Phase 12, build2 11.1; 2026-11-09).** *Conversion:* local north / east / down to WGS 84 through the local tangent plane at the flight's origin fix, exactly (Earth-centred, Earth-fixed coordinates and back), not a flat-Earth scale: the inverse uses Vermeille's closed form (Journal of Geodesy 76, 451-454, 2002), which needs no iteration and is exact near the surface. Pinned by tests against pymap3d 3.1.0 (BSD-2-Clause, a development dependency only) at 400 random origins between 80 deg S and 80 deg N with offsets up to 5 km: latitude and longitude within 1e-9 deg (0.1 mm), height within 0.1 mm, and both directions; the NED to ENU change that ROS needs (REP 103) is in the same module. *What a map point is:* the reported ground point of each valid track-frame (after the footprint and contact corrections), now written to tracks.csv with its 2 x 2 covariance: the propagated covariance of the box's ground point (D-735) plus the footprint offset's variance along the camera heading (D-736). Options for the ellipse: (a) that raw covariance; (b) (a) scaled by the row's calibrated-to-raw range ratio (68 % half-width over sigma) where the run has calibrated intervals (D-737); (c) an ellipse built from the range interval and a bearing sigma. (b) is chosen: it keeps the shape the geometry gives and the size the calibration found (raw range intervals over-cover badly on SynDrone, D-737), and it agrees with the range interval shown beside it; (c) would need a bearing sigma the pipeline does not estimate. Drawn at 95 % (chi-squared, 2 degrees of freedom). Speed and heading appear only where the display rule shows the speed (D-739). *Searched ground:* per frame, the ground seen by the image rows at least 10 deg below the horizon on the image's centre column. Options: the whole image below the horizon (unbounded near the horizon), a fixed distance cap (an invented number), or the depression at which a range can be reliable. The last is chosen: 10 deg is the lower edge of the first depression band whose relative range sigma meets the reliability threshold in `configs/run.yaml` (0.099 against 0.10), so below it every range is flagged uncertain; the limit is read from the config, not set here. Phase 14's detection model will refine where a person can actually be seen. *Format:* GeoJSON (RFC 7946: longitude, latitude; anticlockwise exterior rings; no `crs` member; 7 decimals, about 1 cm), checked by tests. *Viewer:* one self-contained HTML file with the GeoJSON embedded, drawn on a canvas in Web Mercator with a scale bar, a time slider, layer switches and a click panel. Map tiles are off by default. OpenStreetMap's tile policy (operations.osmfoundation.org/policies/tiles, read 2026-11-09) requires visible attribution, forbids offline use and pre-fetching, and does not cover pages opened from a local file, so no tile server is built in: a user may name one (`?tiles=<XYZ template>&attribution=<text>`) and is then responsible for its terms; the viewer requests only the tiles in view and always shows the attribution. People are a class and a track number only (R16). Checked in a browser on AU-AIR clip 35 (`scripts/auair_map.py`): 80 tracks, 1,484 detections, 318 footprints, no console errors.

**D-746 Why the IMM harms moving vehicles (the question left open in D-735; 2026-11-09).** *Diagnosis* (`scripts/syndrone_imm_vehicle_diagnosis.py`, development towns, identical rows; the constant-velocity filter with propagated errors, `v2trk_prop_cv`, against the best of the 30 corrected IMM points for vehicles from 3 m/s, `immgrid_fixveh_25`; 13,097 rows). Where the IMM is more than 1 m/s worse than the constant-velocity filter, it reads slower than the truth on 98.1 % of rows: the stationary model pulls moving cars towards zero. The share of rows reading below half the true speed is 12.4 % against 2.9 %; by track age it is 59 % at up to 5 updates, 13 % at 6 to 10, 7.5 % at 11 to 25, 4.3 % at 26 to 50 and 3.0 % at 51 to 100, and it rises with range (9.9 / 12.1 / 17.9 % below 50, 50 to 100 and 100 to 150 m) and at 20 m camera height (14.9 % against 11.1 to 11.7 %), that is, where the measurement noise is largest against the distance a car moves between updates. The worst-tail gap closes with age (90th-percentile absolute error 5.29 against 4.08 m/s at up to 5 updates, 2.03 against 1.84 at 51 to 100). *Cause:* the reported speed is the IMM's combined estimate (`geometry/imm.py`, `speed_heading` of the moment-matched mixture), and the stationary model's velocity is zero, so its probability scales a moving car's speed down. With decimetre-to-metre position noise and a car moving 0.1 to 0.5 m per update, the likelihoods of "stationary" and "moving" differ little over a few updates, the mode probability stays near its prior (vehicles are stationary 62 % of the time in CARLA's truth, and fitted straight-motion stays last 1.9 s, so probability flows back to the stationary mode at every step), and the mixture is biased low; the starting-mode fix of D-735 removed only part of the start-up effect. The combined estimate is the minimum-mean-square-error estimate under the model, but a speed shown to a person is a different quantity: it should be the speed of the object given whether it is moving. *Remedy to test, written before any run:* option (b) of the IMM output, "conditional": the reported state is the moment-matched mixture of the moving models (constant velocity and turn, renormalised) when their combined probability is at least 0.5, and otherwise zero speed with the full mixture's speed sigma; the filter itself (mixing, update, gating) is unchanged, so only what is reported moves. Option (a), the single most probable model's estimate, is also considered but rejected without a run, because it jumps between the two moving models' headings on turns, which the conditional mixture averages. *Test:* the corrected 30-point vehicle grid (D-735) replayed with the conditional output on the development towns, under D-735's stratified rule as written (a point qualifies only if its 90th-percentile error is no worse than version 1's in every vehicle stratum; among those, the lowest mean of the strata's 90th percentiles); if a point qualifies, the filter per group for vehicles is decided by the held-out test of D-735 on the calibration towns (all three vehicle strata no worse than the constant-velocity filter). People keep the configuration adopted in D-735 unless the same test is run for them. *Result* (`results/syndrone/development/imm_grid_cond.json`, `scripts/syndrone_imm_grid.py --output conditional`, the same 30 points and rows): no point qualifies, and the conditional output is worse than the mixture where it mattered. The best point for vehicles from 3 m/s (gate 0.999, reset 2, `q_stationary` 0) reaches a 90th-percentile error of 5.51 m/s against 4.25 with the mixture output and 2.74 with the constant-velocity filter (version 1 2.76); vehicles 0.1 to 3 m/s 2.67 against 2.43; parked vehicles improve (1.92 against 2.05); moving people get worse (1.54 against 1.29). So the rule keeps **the constant-velocity filter for vehicles** and nothing in the configuration changes; the conditional output stays available (`output: conditional`) but unused. *What this settles:* reporting the speed given the more probable motion class turns a partial pull towards zero into a full one whenever the stationary class wins wrongly, which it does often for moving cars; the fault is therefore in the mode probabilities themselves, not in how the models' estimates are combined. With ground-point noise of decimetres to metres and a car moving 0.1 to 0.5 m between updates, a few updates cannot tell a slow car from a parked one, and CARLA's frequent stops make the stationary prior strong. A filter that waits for evidence (the constant-velocity filter, whose speed is noisy but unbiased) is the more robust choice for vehicles at this measurement quality; the IMM's advantage for people (D-735's held-out test) is unaffected. The question opened in D-735 is closed.

**D-747 ROS 2 node (Phase 12, build2 11.2; 2026-11-09).** *Platform:* ROS 2 Jazzy Jalisco on Ubuntu 24.04 (build2's default; Jazzy is the current long-term release, supported to 2029), in Docker from the official `ros:jazzy-ros-base` image, which is built for x86-64 and ARM64. *One pipeline, two drivers:* the per-frame work of `aerialrange run` (detection, tracking, the range and speed stage, class mapping) moved into `pipeline.run.FramePipeline`, which `run` and the node both call, so the exit criterion (the node's output equal to `aerialrange run`'s) is met by construction and then checked; the move left `run`'s output byte-identical (the synthetic demo, 112 rows, and 40 AU-AIR frames with the D-FINE detector, 86 rows, compared before and after). The comparison runs `aerialrange run` inside the same image, so it does not depend on this; whether results differ between the two environments is a separate question, not claimed here. *Test:* `scripts/ros2_integration.sh` builds the image and, inside it, runs `aerialrange run` as the reference, converts the same frames and flight data into a rosbag2 recording (`make_bag`: image, camera info, odometry and fix with equal stamps, MCAP storage), plays it into the node under `launch_testing`, and requires every frame processed, identical track numbers, classes, validity, reasons, flags and display verdicts, and every number within 1e-6 (relative and absolute). *Results (2026-11-10):* AU-AIR clip 35 (318 frames, 1,484 rows) and SynDrone Town01 at 50 m (60 frames, 190 rows) pass in the x86-64 image; the first 40 frames of clip 35 (86 rows) pass in the ARM64 image under emulation, which shows the node works on ARM64 but not how fast (about 9 s a frame under the emulator).
